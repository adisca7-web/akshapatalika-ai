# ASC 952 — Franchisors

> Industry skill distilled from *Wiley GAAP 2020*, ch. 64 (Specialized Industry GAAP).
> Rules and structure synthesised locally; no source text reproduced.

**Applies to** franchisors — parties who grant business rights to a franchisee who will operate the franchised business. ASC 952 offers **incremental** guidance only (ASC 952-10-05-1).

> **Technical alert.** Maintenance Update 2017-09 simplified ASC 952 and consolidated all of it into **ASC 952-10**. Older references to ASC 952-605 predate that consolidation.

---

## Core model

The single most important thing to know about franchisor accounting is what ASC 952 **no longer does**. Legacy franchise guidance had its own revenue model built around "substantial performance" of initial services. That model is gone: **revenue recognition for franchise fees now runs through ASC 606**, and ASC 952 survives only as incremental guidance around the edges — chiefly cost treatment and repossessions.

So the working question for an initial franchise fee is an ASC 606 question: what are the distinct performance obligations, is the licence of intellectual property **symbolic** (revenue over the licence term) or **functional** (revenue at a point in time), and how is the transaction price allocated between the licence, pre-opening services, training, and equipment?

The two areas where ASC 952 still speaks directly are below.

---

## Subskills

### 1. Franchisor costs
Indirect costs that are **regular and recurring, incurred regardless of sales level, and bearing no relationship to sales**, are **expensed as incurred** (ASC 952-340-25-1, 25-2).

The corollary matters more than the rule: costs that *are* directly related to a specific franchise sale, and that are recoverable, are candidates for capitalisation as contract costs under ASC 340-40 rather than immediate expense. The ASC 952 rule exists to stop franchisors from deferring general overhead against future franchise sales.

### 2. Repossessed franchises
The treatment turns entirely on **whether the fee is refunded** (ASC 952-605-25-17):

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Franchisor **refunds** the fee and obtains the location | **Reverse** previously recognised revenue, in the period of repossession |
| Repossession **without** refund | **No adjustment** to revenue previously recognised |

Where there is no refund: provide for any estimated uncollectible amounts, and record any remaining collected funds as revenue.

The logic is that a refund undoes the original exchange, so the revenue was never earned; a repossession without refund is a separate event — the franchisor keeps what it was paid for services already delivered.

### 3. Recognise an area franchise for what it is
An **area franchise** transfers rights within a geographic area permitting a number of outlets, with decisions on outlet count and location made largely **unilaterally by the franchisee**. The franchisor may sell to an operator, or to an **intermediary franchisee** who then sub-franchises to operators.

That autonomy matters under ASC 606: the franchisor's remaining performance obligations differ sharply from a single-outlet agreement, and the number of outlets is often variable consideration subject to the constraint.

## Decision rules

| If | Then | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Recognising initial or continuing franchise fee revenue | Apply **ASC 606**, not legacy franchise guidance | 952-10 (as amended by MU 2017-09) |
| Cost is regular, recurring, and unrelated to sales | Expense as incurred | 952-340-25-1/25-2 |
| Fee refunded and location repossessed | Reverse previously recognised revenue in the period of repossession | 952-605-25-17 |
| Repossessed without refund | Leave prior revenue alone; provide for uncollectibles; record remaining collections as revenue | 952-605-25-17 |
| Agreement charges no initial or royalty fee | It can **still be a franchise agreement** — fees are not a defining criterion | 952 glossary |
| Area franchise sold to an intermediary | Identify the customer and the remaining obligations carefully under ASC 606 | 952 glossary |

## Anti-patterns

- **Applying the old "substantial performance" model.** Franchise fee revenue is an ASC 606 question now.
- **Recognising the full initial fee on opening day** without allocating transaction price across the licence, training, pre-opening services, and equipment.
- **Deferring general overhead against expected franchise sales.** Regular recurring indirect costs are expensed as incurred.
- **Reversing revenue on every repossession.** Only a refund triggers reversal.
- **Assuming a fee is required** for an arrangement to be a franchise agreement. It is not.
- **Treating the area-franchise intermediary the same as an operator.** Their performance obligations differ.

## Key terms

- **Franchise agreement** — a written business agreement meeting all of: a contractual relationship in force for a specified period; a continuing relation whose purpose is distributing a product, service, or entire business concept within a market area; **both parties contribute resources** (franchisor: trademark, reputation, products, procedures, labour, equipment, or process; franchisee: operating capital plus managerial and operational resources); specified marketing practices, contributions, and operating procedures; an outlet that in most cases requires and supports the franchisee's full-time business activity; and a **common public identity**, usually via shared trade names or trademarks reinforced by advertising. **Payment of an initial or continuing royalty fee is not a necessary criterion.**
- **Area franchise** — an agreement transferring franchise rights within a geographic area permitting a number of outlets, with outlet decisions made largely unilaterally by the franchisee.
- **Continuing franchise fee** — consideration for continuing rights granted by the agreement and for general or specific services during its term.
- **Franchisee / franchisor** — the party granted, and the party granting, the business rights.
