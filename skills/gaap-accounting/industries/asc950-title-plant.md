# ASC 950 — Financial Services: Title Plant

> Industry skill distilled from *Wiley GAAP 2020*, ch. 64 (Specialized Industry GAAP).
> Rules and structure synthesised locally; no source text reproduced.

**Applies to** title insurance companies, title abstract companies, and title agents that use a title plant in their operations (ASC 950-350-15-1).

A **title plant** is a historical record of all matters affecting title to parcels of land in a particular geographic area. Its span depends on regulatory requirements and the minimum information period needed to issue title policies efficiently, and it is updated frequently as current title status is added (ASC 950-350-05-3, 05-5).

---

## Core model

A title plant is the rare capitalised asset that is **not depreciated or amortised at all**. The reasoning is that a properly maintained plant does not wear out — it is continuously refreshed, and the cost of that refreshing is expensed as incurred. Its carrying amount therefore stays flat until something impairs it.

That produces a clean three-way split:

| Activity | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Building** the plant to a usable state | Capitalise |
| **Maintaining** the plant and doing title searches | Expense as incurred |
| **Impairment** | Write down under ASC 360 |

---

## Subskills

### 1. Capitalise construction costs
Capitalise costs directly incurred to construct a title plant, **up to the point the plant can be used to do title searches** (ASC 950-350-25-1, 30-1). Construction cost means obtaining, organising, and summarising historical information for a tract of land.

Only costs **directly related to and traceable to** the construction activities qualify. General overhead does not.

Costs to construct a **backplant** — a title plant antedating the period covered by the existing plant — are also capitalised.

### 2. Record acquisitions
A purchased title plant, backplant, or **undivided interest** in one (the right to joint use) is recorded **at cost as of the date acquired**. Where acquired separately, record at the **fair value of the consideration given** (ASC 950-350-30-2).

### 3. Do not amortise; test for impairment
Capitalised title plant costs are **not amortised or depreciated** unless an impairment occurs (ASC 950-350-35-1).

Indicators that carrying amount may not be recoverable (non-exhaustive, ASC 950-350-35-2):
- Changing legal or statutory requirements
- Economic factors such as changing demand
- Loss of competitive advantage
- **Failure to maintain an up-to-date title plant**
- Circumstances indicating obsolescence, such as abandonment

The fourth is the distinctive one: because the asset is never amortised, letting the plant go stale is precisely what destroys its value, and it is an explicit impairment trigger.

Impairment is measured under **ASC 360** (ASC 950-350-35-3) — the two-step recoverability test using **undiscounted** cash flows first, then fair value.

### 4. Expense operating and conversion costs
- Title plant **maintenance** and **conducting title searches**: expense currently (ASC 950-350-25-2).
- Costs to **convert the record from one storage and retrieval system to another**, or to modify the current system, **may not be capitalised as title plant**. They may, however, be **separately capitalised and amortised** by a systematic and rational method (ASC 950-350-25-3, 25-4).

That distinction keeps the never-amortised title plant balance clean: technology costs live in a separate, amortising asset.

### 5. Report sales by type
The sale of a title plant is reported separately, and the amount depends on what was sold (ASC 950-350-40-1):

| Terms of sale | Amount reported | Framework |
|---|---|---|
| **a.** Sale of title plant with waiver of all rights to future use | Amount received **less adjusted cost** of the title plant | ASC 610-20 |
| **b.** Sale of an **undivided ownership interest** (rights to future joint use) | Amount received **less a pro rata portion** of adjusted cost | ASC 610-20 |
| **c.** Sale of **copies** of the title plant, or the right to use it | Full amount | **ASC 606** if a contract with a customer; otherwise ASC 610-20 |

The pattern: selling the asset itself (a, b) is a derecognition event under ASC 610-20 measured net of cost; selling *access* (c) is ordinary revenue under ASC 606 with no cost relief against the plant.

## Decision rules

| If | Then | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Cost is directly traceable to building the plant, pre-usability | Capitalise | 950-350-25-1, 30-1 |
| Plant becomes usable for title searches | Stop capitalising construction cost | 950-350-25-1 |
| Cost is maintenance or a title search | Expense currently | 950-350-25-2 |
| Building a backplant | Capitalise on the same basis | 950-350-30-1 |
| Acquiring a plant or undivided interest | Record at cost on acquisition date; separately acquired, at fair value of consideration | 950-350-30-2 |
| Period end, no impairment indicator | **No amortisation or depreciation** | 950-350-35-1 |
| Plant not kept up to date | Impairment indicator — test it | 950-350-35-2 |
| Impairment indicated | Apply ASC 360's two-step test | 950-350-35-3 |
| Converting storage/retrieval systems | Not title plant; may capitalise separately and amortise systematically | 950-350-25-3/25-4 |
| Selling the plant outright | Proceeds less adjusted cost, per ASC 610-20 | 950-350-40-1 |
| Selling an undivided interest | Proceeds less **pro rata** adjusted cost | 950-350-40-1 |
| Selling copies or use rights to a customer | ASC 606 revenue | 950-350-40-1 |

## Anti-patterns

- **Amortising the title plant.** It is explicitly not amortised or depreciated absent impairment — a genuine exception to normal long-lived asset accounting.
- **Capitalising maintenance.** Keeping the plant current is an expense, even though it is what preserves the asset's value.
- **Capitalising system conversion costs into the title plant balance.** They go to a separate amortising asset.
- **Capitalising untraceable overhead** into construction cost.
- **Continuing to capitalise after the plant is usable** for title searches.
- **Assuming no impairment because there is no amortisation.** Staleness is an explicit trigger, and ASC 360 applies in full.
- **Relieving title plant cost when selling copies or use rights.** That is ASC 606 revenue; the asset stays.

## Key terms

- **Title plant** — a historical record of all matters affecting title to land in a geographic area; years covered vary with regulatory requirements and the minimum period needed to issue policies efficiently.
- **Backplant** — a title plant antedating the period covered by the existing title plant.
- **Undivided interest** — the right to joint use of a title plant.
