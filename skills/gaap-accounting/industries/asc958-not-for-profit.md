# ASC 958 — Not-for-Profit Entities

> Industry skill distilled from *Wiley GAAP 2020*, ch. 64 (Specialized Industry GAAP).
> Rules and structure synthesised locally; no source text reproduced.

**Applies to nongovernmental** not-for-profit entities (ASC 958-10-05-01): churches and religious entities, colleges and universities, health care entities, libraries, museums, performing arts entities, civic and fraternal entities, federated fund-raising entities, professional and trade associations, social clubs, research entities, cemeteries, arboretums, and zoos.

**Excluded** (ASC 958-10-15-5): entities existing to provide dividends, lower costs, or other economic benefits **directly and proportionately to members, participants, or owners** — mutual insurance companies, credit unions, farm and utility cooperatives, and employee benefit plans. **Governmental** NFPs follow GASB.

All FASB Codification pronouncements apply to NFPs unless a pronouncement excludes them.

---

## Core model

Three structural differences from business entities drive everything:

1. **No profit motive, so no bottom-line performance measure.** The change in net assets is not "net income". Performance information is supplied instead by reporting **revenues and expenses gross rather than net**, and by classifying expenses by the **mission-related programs and supporting activities** they sustain rather than by natural classification.
2. **No comprehensive income distinction.** Because the bottom line is not a performance measure, all revenues, expenses, gains, and losses appear in a **single statement of activities** — there is no separate OCI statement.
3. **Contributions exist**, a transaction type with no business counterpart, frequently carrying donor-imposed restrictions that shape what services the entity can offer.

**Net assets are split into exactly two mutually exclusive classes**: **with donor restrictions** and **without donor restrictions**.

### The distinction that governs timing

| | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Donor-imposed restriction** | Affects **classification only** — which net asset class the contribution increases. **Does not change the timing** of recognition. |
| **Donor-imposed condition** | Affects **timing**. A conditional transfer **is not yet a contribution** and is not recognised until the conditions are **substantially met**. |

A **donor-imposed condition** requires both (ASC 958-605-25-5A): **one or more barriers** to be overcome before the recipient is entitled to the assets, **and** a **right of return** of assets transferred (or release of the promisor from its obligation).

**Ambiguity defaults to conditional**: if the donor's stipulations do not clearly state whether the gift depends on a stipulation, and the ambiguity cannot be resolved by communicating with the donor or examining the circumstances, the transfer is **presumed conditional**.

## Subskills

### 1. Present the required statements
All NFPs present a **statement of financial position**, a **statement of activities**, and a **statement of cash flows** (ASC 958-205-05-5).

**Statement of financial position** reports totals for assets, liabilities, net assets, **net assets with donor restrictions**, and **net assets without donor restrictions** (958-210-45-2). Disclose the nature and extent of donor restrictions on the face or in the notes, plus: the extent to which the balance sheet comprises **financial assets**, their **one-year liquidity**, and any **limitations on their use**.

**Statement of activities** classifies revenues, expenses, gains, and losses into the two net asset classes, reporting the change in each and in total (958-220-45-1). **Reclassifications** — events that simultaneously increase one class and decrease another — are reported **separately**; the classic case is a release of restrictions when a long-lived asset is purchased fulfilling a donor restriction (958-220-45-3).

**Fund accounting** is an internal tool for tracking compliance with donor restrictions and board designations. For **external reporting**, a fund may fall entirely in one net asset class or need allocating across classes.

### 2. Recognise contributions
Contributions are recognised as revenue **at the time of the gift, measured at the fair value of the contributed assets** (958-605-25-2).

**Unconditional promises to give** are recognised when the promise is made and received, provided there is **sufficient evidence in verifiable documentation — written, audio, or video** (958-605-25-8). Where payments are due in future periods, the promise carries an **implied time restriction expiring on the due date**, so unless circumstances show the donor intended to support current-period activities, it increases **restricted** net assets. Measure using a **present value technique**; **short-term promises due in less than one year may be reported at net realisable value**.

**Conditional promises** are **not recognised** until conditions are substantially met, but **must be disclosed** in the notes (958-605-25-11).

**Contributions held by others**: a beneficiary recognises contributions held on its behalf by an agent, trustee, or intermediary. If the assets were transferred subject to an unmet condition, the beneficiary recognises **nothing**. With an **unconditional right** to cash flows from a charitable trust or asset pool, the beneficiary recognises its rights **when the beneficial interest is created**, measured at the **present value of estimated expected cash flows**. If beneficiary and recipient are **financially interrelated**, the beneficiary instead uses a method **similar to the equity method** (958-20-25-2).

**Donated goods and services**: donated assets, services, and use of facilities are recognised at fair value with a corresponding revenue, and forgiveness of a liability by a donor is likewise contribution revenue. (Note: NFPs report these within **net assets**, not retained earnings — the source text's illustration mislabels the entity as for-profit and refers to retained earnings, which does not apply to an NFP.)

**Collections**: an entity that maintains works of art, historical treasures, and similar assets **in collections as defined** does **not recognise gifts added to its collections unless it also capitalises its collections** (958-605-25-19). Gifts **not** added to collections, or items given to entities that do not maintain collections meeting the definition, **are** recognised as revenue at fair value (958-605-25-18).

**Exchange transactions** follow other topics — principally **ASC 606** (958-605-25-1) — and are generally classified as **unrestricted**.

### 3. Investments and endowments
- **Debt securities** are reported at **fair value** (958-320-25-1). Although the same securities are covered by ASC 320 for businesses, **gains and losses — realised and unrealised — are reported in the statement of activities**, not through OCI.
- A **contributed equity security** is recognised as an asset and as revenue or gain in the period received (958-321-25-1).
- Debt securities are reported at **cost on acquisition if purchased**, or at **fair value if received as a contribution or through an agency transaction** (958-325-30-1).

**Endowment funds** may be **donor-restricted** (perpetual or term) or **board-designated** (quasi-endowment / funds functioning as endowment). Because a donor may restrict **each source separately**, the **original gift, investment income, and investment gains and losses must each be examined individually**. Each source is unrestricted unless restricted by the donor or by a law extending the donor's restriction to it.

Consequently, an endowment created by the **governing board** from an unrestricted bequest is **entirely unrestricted** — no donor was involved.

**UPMIFA default**: unless the gift instrument states otherwise, classify endowment fund assets as **donor-restricted until appropriated for expenditure** by the organisation (958-205-05-10).

**Endowment disclosures** (958-205-50-1B, 50-2(c)):
- The governing board's **interpretation of the law** supporting net asset classification of donor-restricted endowment funds
- **Endowment spending policies**, including interpretation of the ability to spend from **underwater** funds
- **Endowment investment policies**
- The **composition of the endowment by net asset class** at period end — in total and by fund, showing **donor-restricted separately from board-designated**
- A **reconciliation of beginning and ending balances**, in total and by net asset class
- The **aggregate deficiency** for all donor-restricted endowment funds where fair value at the reporting date is **below the level required by donor stipulations or law**

### 4. Split-interest agreements
A donor transfers assets to the NFP or to a charitable trust, requiring the benefits of ownership to be **split among two or more beneficiaries** (958-220-20). Examples: charitable gift annuities, annuity trusts, charitable remainder unitrusts, charitable lead trusts, and pooled (life) income funds.

| Who holds the assets | Accounting |
|---|---|
| **A third party** (e.g. a bank) holds them; NFP is a beneficiary with **unconditional rights** to cash flows | Recognise the **interest as an asset and contribution revenue**, at **fair value**, usually the **present value of cash flows to be received** (958-30-25-17) |
| **The NFP** holds the assets and is a beneficiary | Report **fair value of assets received** as assets, and the **actuarially computed present value of payments to other beneficiaries** as a liability. **The difference is the contribution** (958-30-25-4) |
| **Revocable** agreement | **Not recorded unless the NFP holds the assets**; assets held as trustee under a revocable agreement are recognised as **refundable advances at fair value** (958-30-25-2) |

Each year, **recompute the liability** based on revaluation of amounts payable, expected beneficiary lives, and other actuarial assumptions (958-30-35-6).

**Classification**: most split-interest net assets are **restricted**, by time (distributions not yet due, or the contribution cannot be used until the beneficiary's death or a specified date) and/or purpose. Where the donor permanently restricts use — for example requiring the remainder to create a permanent endowment — classification follows. But where the contribution portion **can be spent immediately without restriction**, as with some gift annuities, it is **unrestricted** (958-30-45-2).

### 5. Transfers received as agent, trustee, or intermediary
A donor transfers assets to a **recipient entity** that agrees to use them on behalf of, or transfer them to, a **beneficiary** named by the donor.

**Default: the recipient reports a liability**, reduced as it spends on the beneficiary's behalf or transfers the assets or their return (958-20-25-1).

**Three exceptions:**

1. **Variance power**: where the donor **explicitly grants** the recipient the **unilateral power to redirect** the assets to an entity other than the specified beneficiary — without contacting the donor, beneficiary, or any interested party — the **recipient recognises contribution revenue**, and the beneficiary does **not** recognise potential distributions until it has unconditional rights (958-30-25-18). Variance power **must be granted by the transferring instrument**.

2. **Financially interrelated entities**: the recipient reports **contribution revenue**, and the beneficiary recognises its interest in the recipient's net assets using a method **similar to the equity method**. Entities are financially interrelated if **both** (958-20-05-1): one can **influence the operating and financial decisions** of the other, **and** one has an **ongoing economic interest** in the other's net assets. Influence may be shown by being affiliates under ASC 850, considerable board representation, charter or bylaw limits confining activities to the other's benefit, or an agreement permitting active participation in policymaking (958-20-15-2). A typical case is a foundation existing to raise, hold, and invest assets for a specific supported beneficiary.

3. **Nonfinancial assets**: where the assets received are **donated materials, supplies, or other nonfinancial assets**, the recipient may elect either to **report a liability concurrent with the asset**, or **not report the transaction at all**. This is an **accounting policy**, applied consistently and **disclosed**.

**Transfers that are not contributions** — revocable, repayable, or reciprocal — are recognised as **liabilities** where any of these apply: the transfer is subject to the transferor's right to **redirect** the assets; it is accompanied by the transferor's **conditional promise to give**; the transferor **controls** the recipient and specifies an **unaffiliated** beneficiary; or the transferor specifies **itself or its affiliate** as beneficiary in a transfer that is not an equity transaction.

**Equity transactions** require all three (958-20-25-4): the transferor specifies **itself or its affiliate** as beneficiary; transferor and recipient are **financially interrelated**; and **neither the transferor nor its affiliate expects repayment** of the transferred assets (investment return may still be paid). Equity transactions are reported by the recipient as a **separate line item in the statement of activities**.

### 6. Property, plant, and equipment
NFPs **must depreciate** land improvements, buildings, and equipment (958-360-35-1).

**Exception** — depreciation need not be recognised for works of art, historical treasures, and similar assets where the NFP can demonstrate **both** (958-360-35-3):
1. The asset **individually** has cultural, historical, or aesthetic value **worth maintaining in perpetuity**, and
2. The organisation has the **ability to protect and preserve that value essentially undiminished**.

**Depreciation is a natural expense classification and must be allocated** to programs and supporting activities in the functional expense report.

### 7. Report expenses functionally
Expenses are recognised as decreases in **net assets without donor restrictions**. **Financing an expense with donor-restricted resources does not make the expense restricted** — it **releases** the restriction, producing a reclassification.

Expenses **must** be reported by **functional classification**, on the face of the statement of activities or in the notes (958-720-45-2):
- **Program services** — mission-related activities delivering goods and services to clients, customers, or members; the major purpose and output of the organisation.
- **Supporting activities** — everything else, commonly **fund raising** and **management and general**.

An analysis by **natural classification** (salaries, benefits, rent, depreciation) is **encouraged but not required** for most NFPs — it helps readers understand the mix of fixed and discretionary costs. **Only voluntary health and welfare entities must report both**, in a **matrix format statement of functional expenses** (958-205-45-6).

### 8. Joint activities
A **joint activity** conducts fund raising together with a program or management and general function — for example a direct mail campaign carrying both health guidance and a donation request.

Because management is often judged on the proportion of spending going to programs, there is a natural incentive to characterise joint costs as program expense. ASC 958-720 therefore **begins with the presumption that all costs of a joint activity are fund-raising expense**.

To overcome the presumption, **all three criteria** must be met: **purpose**, **audience**, and **content**. Each of the first two contains further tests — purpose includes a **call to action** test, a **compensation or fees** test, a **similar scale and same medium** test, and an **other evidence** test; audience includes a **prior donor** test, an **ability and likelihood to contribute** test, and a **need to use or reasonable potential for use** test. Failing one sub-test usually fails the criterion.

| Outcome | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **All three met** | Costs identifiable with a particular function are charged to it; **joint costs are allocated** between fund raising and the appropriate program or management and general function (958-720-45-29) |
| **Any criterion failed** | **All** costs of the joint activity are reported as **fund-raising expense** — including costs that would have been program or management and general in another activity |

**One exception to the all-to-fundraising rule**: costs of goods or services provided in an **exchange transaction** within the joint activity (a quid pro quo contribution) go to **cost of goods sold**, not fund raising — for example items sold at a fund-raising auction or meals served at a fund-raising dinner.

**Joint costs** are those not directly identifiable with a particular component: salaries, professional fees, paper, printing, postage, event advertising, telephones, broadcast airtime, facility rentals.

The **allocation method must be rational, systematic, and produce a reasonable allocation**, applied consistently for similar facts. No method is mandated; three are illustrated — **physical units**, **relative direct cost**, and **standalone joint-cost-allocation** (958-720-45-54).

**Disclose** (958-720-50-2): the types of activities incurring joint costs, a statement that costs have been allocated, the **total joint costs allocated**, and the portion allocated to **each functional expense category**. Disclosure of joint costs per type of activity is encouraged.

### 9. Mergers and acquisitions
A combination of NFPs cannot be assumed to be an **exchange of commensurate value** — acquired NFPs lack owners seeking a return, and a governing body may put **mission effectiveness ahead of maximising price**. So first determine whether there was an exchange of commensurate value, or a **contribution inherent in the transaction** reported under ASC 958-605.

An inherent contribution is measured as the **excess of net fair values of identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed over the fair value of consideration exchanged**. Where the acquired entity is a **business enterprise**, it is the excess of the acquired business's fair value over its cost. **The key difference: no goodwill is recognised in most contributions of not-for-profit entities**, though it is for business entities.

In the rare case where the fair value of **liabilities assumed exceeds** that of identifiable assets acquired, the acquirer initially recognises the excess as an **unidentifiable intangible asset (goodwill)**.

| Combination | Method |
|---|---|
| **Merger** — two or more NFPs **cede control to a new entity** | **Carryover method** (958-805-25-3): carry forward assets and liabilities at **carrying amounts**, less the effects of intra-entity transactions (25-6). **No fair value changes recognised.** The new entity's financial history **begins on the merger date** — prior results of the merging entities are not reported. |
| **Acquisition** | **Acquisition method**, with NFP-specific exceptions below |

**Acquisition method exceptions:**
- **No acquired donor relationship** may be recognised as an intangible asset (958-805-25-22).
- Where the acquirer's policy is **not to capitalise collections**, it does **not** recognise acquired collection items as assets. Instead it recognises the **cost of purchased collection items as a decrease in the appropriate net asset class** in the statement of activities and as a **cash outflow from investing activities**, and does **not recognise the fair value of contributed collection items** (958-805-25-23).
- **Conditional promises to give**: recognise the promise **only if the conditions are substantially met as of the acquisition date**; otherwise recognise a transfer of assets with a conditional promise as a **refundable advance** (958-805-25-26).
- **Goodwill expensed**: where the acquirer expects the acquiree's operations to be supported **primarily by contributions and returns on investments** — that is, those sources are expected to **significantly exceed all other revenue** — the amount that would otherwise be goodwill is **recognised as an expense on the acquisition date** (958-805-25-29).
- An acquisition always contains an **inherent contribution received**, because the acquirer receives net assets without a corresponding transfer of consideration (958-805-25-31).

**Disclosures**: mergers — reasons for the merger, significant adjustments to conform accounting policies, and intra-entity balance eliminations. Acquisitions — reasons, the factors making up goodwill recognised or the equivalent amount expensed, and the fair value of consideration transferred (958-805-50-11).

### 10. Determine the reporting entity
**Control** is the direct or indirect ability to determine the direction of management and policies through ownership, contract, or otherwise. An **economic interest** exists if the other entity **holds or utilises significant resources that must be used for the reporting organisation's purposes**, or the reporting organisation is **responsible for the other's liabilities** (958-810-20).

- Controlling financial interest via **majority voting interest in a for-profit entity** (other than a limited partnership) → follow **ASC 810** (958-810-25-2).
- Control of a related but separate NFP through a **majority voting interest in its board** → also follow ASC 810 (958-810-25-3).
- **Significant influence** (generally 20% to under 50% of voting interest) → apply **ASC 323** or report the investment at **fair value** (958-810-25-4C).

**Control and economic interest matrix** (958-810-25-4):

| Control | Economic interest | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Majority **voting ownership** interest | Either | **Consolidate** |
| Majority voting interest in the **board** | **Yes** | **Consolidate** |
| Majority voting interest in the **board** | **No** | **Consolidation prohibited**; disclosure required |
| Via **contract or affiliation agreement** | **Yes** | **Permitted but not required** (encouraged where consolidation would be meaningful) |
| Via **contract or affiliation agreement** | **No** | **Consolidation prohibited**; disclosure required |
| **No control** | **Yes** | **Consolidation prohibited**; disclosure required |
| **No control** | **No** | **Consolidation prohibited**; no disclosure required |

Where consolidation is **permitted but not taken**, disclose the other organisation's identity, the nature of the relationship, and **summarised financial data**, in addition to ASC 850 information (958-810-50-4). Where consolidation is **prohibited**, disclose the ASC 850 information.

## Decision rules

| If | Then | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Donor imposes a **restriction** | Affects **classification only**; recognise now | 958-605-25-2 |
| Donor imposes a **condition** (barrier + right of return) | **Do not recognise** until substantially met | 958-605-25-5A |
| Donor's stipulation is ambiguous and unresolvable | **Presume conditional** | 958-605 |
| Unconditional promise, payment due in a future period | Restricted (implied time restriction); measure at **present value** | 958-605-25-8 |
| Unconditional promise due in **under one year** | May report at **net realisable value** | 958-605-25-8 |
| Conditional promise to give | Not recognised, but **disclosed** | 958-605-25-11 |
| Gift added to a collection, collections **not** capitalised | **Do not recognise** | 958-605-25-19 |
| Gift **not** added to a collection | Recognise at fair value | 958-605-25-18 |
| Recipient holds assets for a donor-named beneficiary | **Liability** | 958-20-25-1 |
| Donor grants **variance power** | Recipient recognises **contribution revenue** | 958-30-25-18 |
| Recipient and beneficiary are **financially interrelated** | Recipient: revenue; beneficiary: **equity-method-like** interest | 958-20-05-1 |
| Donated **nonfinancial** assets received as agent | Policy election: liability **or** no recognition — applied consistently and disclosed | 958-20 |
| Revocable split-interest agreement, NFP holds assets | **Refundable advance** at fair value | 958-30-25-2 |
| Endowment gift instrument silent | Donor-restricted **until appropriated for expenditure** | 958-205-05-10 |
| Board creates an endowment from unrestricted funds | **Unrestricted** — no donor involved | 958-205 |
| Art/treasure has perpetual value **and** is preservable | **Depreciation not required** | 958-360-35-3 |
| Expense financed with restricted resources | Expense is **unrestricted**; restriction is **released** | 958-720 |
| Joint activity fails **any** of purpose/audience/content | **All** costs to fund raising | 958-720-45-29 |
| Exchange element within a failed joint activity | To **cost of goods sold**, not fund raising | 958-720-45-29 |
| Two NFPs cede control to a new entity | **Carryover method**; history starts at merger date | 958-805-25-3, 25-6 |
| NFP acquires another entity | Acquisition method; **no donor-relationship intangible** | 958-805-25-22 |
| Acquiree to be supported mainly by contributions and investment returns | **Expense** the would-be goodwill on the acquisition date | 958-805-25-29 |
| Control via board majority but **no** economic interest | **Consolidation prohibited**; disclose | 958-810-25-4 |
| Control via contract **and** economic interest | Consolidation **permitted, not required** | 958-810-25-4 |

## Anti-patterns

- **Treating a donor restriction as delaying recognition.** Restrictions affect classification; only **conditions** affect timing.
- **Recognising a conditional promise** before the barriers are substantially met — or failing to disclose it.
- **Resolving donor ambiguity in favour of recognition.** The presumption is conditional.
- **Reporting investment gains through OCI.** NFPs have no OCI; gains and losses go to the statement of activities.
- **Classifying a board-designated endowment as restricted.** No donor, no donor restriction.
- **Classifying endowment gains as unrestricted by default.** Each source — gift, income, gains — is examined separately, and UPMIFA holds them restricted until appropriated.
- **Spending endowment assets before appropriation** without recognising the default classification rule.
- **Treating an expense as restricted** because restricted resources funded it. It releases the restriction instead.
- **Allocating joint costs to program** without passing all three of purpose, audience, and content — and each of their sub-tests.
- **Charging quid pro quo exchange costs to fund raising** when a joint activity fails the criteria.
- **Recognising goodwill in a merger of NFPs**, or applying fair value under the carryover method.
- **Recognising an acquired donor list as an intangible asset.**
- **Consolidating on board control alone** without an economic interest — that combination prohibits consolidation.
- **Depreciating all collection items.** The perpetual-value-plus-preservability exception may apply.
- **Reporting NFP results as retained earnings.** NFPs report **net assets** in two classes.

## Key terms

- **Net assets** — excess of assets over liabilities, split into **with** and **without donor restrictions**.
- **Designated net assets** — net assets without donor restrictions subject to **self-imposed** limits by governing board action.
- **Donor-imposed restriction** — a stipulation more specific than the broad limits arising from the NFP's nature, environment, or articles/bylaws. May be **temporary** (a date, a program, acquiring buildings) or **perpetual**.
- **Endowment fund** — an established fund providing income for the NFP's maintenance, with or without donor restrictions.
- **Spending rate** — the portion of total investment return used for current fiscal needs, usually an amount or percentage of a **moving average market value**, set to balance current support against protecting endowment purchasing power.
- **Agent** — acts for and on behalf of another; a recipient is an agent if it agrees to use or transfer assets for a specified beneficiary, if it solicits assets specifically for a beneficiary, or if the beneficiary **can compel** distributions.
- **Intermediary** — facilitates a transfer between donor and donee but is neither agent, trustee, donee, nor donor.
- **Trustee** — has a duty to hold and manage assets for a **specified** beneficiary under a charitable trust agreement. NFPs organised under trust law holding assets for a **community or broad group** are **not** trustees in this sense.
- **Collections** — works of art, historical treasures, or similar assets that are (1) **held for public exhibition, education, or research** rather than financial gain, (2) **protected, unencumbered, cared for, and preserved**, and (3) subject to a policy requiring **sale proceeds to be used to acquire other collection items**.
- **Natural expense classification** — grouping by the kind of economic benefit received: salaries, benefits, professional services, supplies, interest, rent, utilities, depreciation.
