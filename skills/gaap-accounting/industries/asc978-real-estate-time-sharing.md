# ASC 978 — Real Estate: Time-Sharing Activities

> Industry skill distilled from *Wiley GAAP 2020*, ch. 64 (Specialized Industry GAAP).
> Rules and structure synthesised locally; no source text reproduced.

**Applies to** a seller's accounting for real estate time-sharing transactions, where buyers acquire the right to use property — typically resort condominiums or vacation property — for a fixed number of weeks per year (**intervals**).

Covered transaction types:
- **Fee simple** — nonreversionary title and ownership pass to the buyer or to a special-purpose entity
- Title and ownership of all or part of the real estate **remain with the seller**
- Title passes to the buyer and **subsequently reverts** to the seller or transfers to a third party
- Transactions by a **time-share reseller**

---

## Core model

Time-sharing has four defining economic features that together drive the accounting (ASC 978-10-05-5):

1. **Volume-based, homogeneous sales** — many near-identical units, so portfolio-level estimation is both possible and necessary.
2. **Seller financing** — the seller is also the lender, so credit risk stays on its books.
3. **Relatively high selling and marketing costs** — a large cost base competing for capitalisation.
4. **On default, the seller recovers the interval and the buyer forfeits some principal** — the seller gets the asset *back* and keeps part of the money.

That fourth feature is what makes time-sharing distinctive. In an ordinary credit sale, default is a bad debt. Here, default returns saleable inventory to the seller, who can sell the same interval again. Uncollectibility is therefore a structural, recurring feature to be estimated up front, not an exception.

**Classification**: time-share transactions are accounted for as **nonretail land sales**, and are **excluded** from certain provisions otherwise applicable to incidental rental operations.

## Subskills

### 1. Account for incidental operations
Rental and other operations during holding periods — including **sampler programs** and **minivacations** — are accounted for as **incidental operations**. Any **excess of revenue over costs is recorded as a reduction of inventory cost**, not as revenue (ASC 978-330-35-3).

Any excess of *costs over revenue* is charged to expense. The asymmetry is deliberate: incidental operations may reduce the carrying amount of inventory but may never create profit.

### 2. Handle complex structures — VIEs, points, vacation clubs
Time-sharing special-purpose entities (now variable interest entities), points systems, and vacation clubs use the **same profit recognition guidance as simpler structures**, provided the time-sharing interest **has been sold to the end user** (ASC 978-810-25-3).

For balance sheet presentation, treat a VIE as **lacking economic substance and established to facilitate sales** when all of these hold:
- The VIE structure is **legally required** in order to sell intervals to a class of nonresident customers, **and**
- The VIE has **no assets other than the time-sharing intervals**, **and**
- The VIE has **no debt**.

In that case the seller presents interests in the VIE **not yet sold to end users as time-sharing inventory** on its own balance sheet — looking through the entity rather than consolidating a shell.

Where those conditions are not met, apply ASC 810, ASC 323, and ASC 321 (ASC 978-810-25-4).

### 3. Identify the arrangement form
The form determines whether and when title passes, which drives the revenue model:

| Form | Title | Buyer's right |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed time** | Passes by deed | A **specific** period, generally a specific week |
| **Floating time** | Passes by deed | **Not** limited to a specific period |
| **Fractional interest** | Partial ownership | Larger annual blocks, e.g. three weeks or more |
| **Undivided interest** | Tenant-in-common in a unit or entire improved property | Assigned a specific period, plus a specific unit if the interest is in the entire property |
| **Tenancy-for-years** | Qualified right of possession for a term | **Reverts** to seller or a third party at the end |
| **Points / vacation club** | Vacation credits | Redeemable across sites; points required vary by unit type, size, location, season |

### 4. Track projects by phase
A **phase** is a contractually or physically distinguishable portion of a project, distinguished by shared characteristics: units declared or legally registered for sale, units linked to an owners' association, units to be constructed in a particular period, or how the developer plans to build.

Phasing matters because cost allocation and inventory relief operate at the phase level. Examples: a hotel converted to time-share one floor at a time while unconverted units continue to be rented; or several buildings constructed on a property over an extended period.

## Decision rules

| If | Then | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Classifying a time-share sale | Account for it as a **nonretail land sale** | 978-10 |
| Running sampler programs, minivacations, or holding-period rentals | Incidental operations: excess revenue **reduces inventory cost** | 978-330-35-3 |
| Incidental operation costs exceed its revenue | Charge the excess to expense | 978-330-35-3 |
| Interval sold to the end user through a VIE, points system, or club | Same profit recognition as a simple structure | 978-810-25-3 |
| VIE is legally required, holds only intervals, and has no debt | Present unsold interests as **time-sharing inventory** | 978-810-25-3 |
| VIE fails those conditions | Apply ASC 810 / 323 / 321 | 978-810-25-4 |
| Buyer defaults | Seller recovers the interval; buyer forfeits some principal — anticipate this in estimates | 978-10-05-5 |
| Arrangement is tenancy-for-years | Recognise that the interest **reverts**; it is not a permanent transfer | 978 glossary |

## Anti-patterns

- **Recognising incidental operation profit as revenue.** It reduces inventory cost; it can never create income.
- **Treating buyer defaults as ordinary bad debts.** Default returns saleable inventory and forfeits principal — a structural feature to estimate up front, given volume-based homogeneous sales.
- **Consolidating a substanceless sales-facilitation VIE** that is legally required, holds only intervals, and has no debt. Present the unsold interests as inventory instead.
- **Applying a different profit model to points systems or vacation clubs.** Once the interest reaches the end user, the model is the same.
- **Ignoring phases** when allocating project cost and relieving inventory.
- **Treating floating time as if it were fixed time.** Both pass by deed, but the buyer's rights — and the seller's remaining obligations — differ.
- **Applying incidental-rental-operation provisions** that ASC 978 explicitly excludes.

## Key terms

- **Interval** (also time-share, time-sharing interest) — the specific period, generally a week, a unit is available to a particular customer.
- **Fixed time / floating time** — deeded ownership with, or without, a specific annual period.
- **Fractional interest** — partial ownership covering larger annual blocks, typically three weeks or more.
- **Points** — purchased vacation credits redeemable for occupancy at various sites, priced by unit type, size, location, and season.
- **Phase** — a contractually or physically distinguishable portion of a project.
- **Tenancy-for-years** (estate-for-years, term-for-years) — a qualified right of possession for a number of years, reverting afterwards.
- **Time-sharing special-purpose entity** — a corporation or trust to which a seller transfers time-sharing real estate for stock, membership, or beneficial interests.
