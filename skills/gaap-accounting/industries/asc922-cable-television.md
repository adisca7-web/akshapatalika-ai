# ASC 922 — Entertainment: Cable Television

> Industry skill distilled from *Wiley GAAP 2020*, ch. 64 (Specialized Industry GAAP).
> Rules and structure synthesised locally; no source text reproduced.

**Applies to** costs and expenses of cable television systems **during the prematurity period** (ASC 922-10-05-1). Outside that window, ordinary GAAP governs — this topic exists only to solve the problem of a system that is simultaneously under construction and in service.

---

## Core model

A cable system does not switch cleanly from "building" to "operating". Operators energise part of the network and start billing early subscribers while construction continues elsewhere. Normal cost accounting breaks here: expensing everything understates the asset being built, and capitalising everything overstates it once subscribers are paying.

ASC 922 resolves this with a **time-boxed hybrid**. During the prematurity period, costs are split between current operations (expensed) and future operations (capitalised), using a fraction recomputed **every month** as the subscriber base grows. When the period ends, normal accounting resumes.

---

## Subskills

### 1. Establish the prematurity period
This must be fixed **before the first subscriber revenue is recognised**, and it is close to irrevocable.

- **Begins** when revenue from the first subscriber is recognised.
- **Ends** based on plans for completing the first major construction period, or on reaching a specified predetermined subscriber level at which no further investment is required other than cable television plant.
- **Presumed not to exceed two years.**
- Once established, **may not be changed except in highly unusual circumstances** (ASC 922-360-25-1).

That irrevocability is the point: management cannot extend the capitalisation window when a build runs late.

### 2. Decide whether a portion accounts separately
A distinct portion of a system in its own prematurity period accounts separately if it is characterised by **a majority** of these differences (ASC 922-360-25-3):

- Accounting — separate forecasts and budgets
- Investment decisions — separate ROI and breakeven analysis
- Geographical — separate franchise area
- Mechanical — separate equipment
- Timing — separate inception of construction or marketing

Where a portion qualifies, it is charged whole-system costs **only if those costs are directly traceable to it** (ASC 922-360-25-4), and separate projections are developed for it.

### 3. Classify costs during the period

| Cost | Treatment | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Cable television plant | Capitalise as usual | 922-360-25-4 |
| General and administrative expense | **Period cost** — expense | 922-360-25-4/25-5 |
| Subscriber-related costs | **Period cost** — expense | 922-360-25-5 |
| Initial subscriber installation costs | Capitalise; depreciate over a period **not exceeding** the cable plant depreciation period | 922-360-25-7 |
| System costs benefiting the completed system and roughly constant regardless of subscriber count (e.g. programming) | **Split**: portion benefiting current operations expensed, portion allocable to future operations capitalised; assess each **separately for recoverability** | 922-360-35-1 |
| Interest during the period | Capitalise per ASC 835-20, **capped at actual interest cost for the period** | 835-20 |

Subscriber-related costs are broad — billing and collection, bad debts, mailings, repairs and maintenance of taps and connections, franchise fees tied to revenue or subscriber count, general and administrative system costs such as the system manager's salary and office rent, programming for additional marketing channels or per-channel/per-programme services, and **direct selling costs**.

Direct selling costs are commissions, the non-commission portion of a salesperson's compensation for obtaining new subscribers, local advertising targeted at subscriber acquisition, and document processing for new subscribers. They **exclude** supervisory and administrative expense and indirect costs such as rent and facilities.

### 4. Compute prematurity-period depreciation
Current depreciation and amortisation are determined by applying a fraction, **recalculated each month** of the prematurity period, to total monthly system costs (ASC 922-360-35-3). The fraction scales recognition to how far the subscriber base has progressed toward its target, so expense rises as the system fills.

### 5. Account for franchise application costs
A franchise is a right granted by a municipality to serve its residents.

- **Successful application** → capitalise; amortise as an intangible asset over the life of the franchise agreement (ASC 922-350-25-3).
- **Unsuccessful or abandoned** → charge all application costs to expense.

## Decision rules

| If | Then | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| First subscriber revenue is about to be recognised | Fix the prematurity period start and end **now** | 922-360-25-1 |
| Construction overruns the planned period | Do **not** extend the period absent highly unusual circumstances | 922-360-25-1 |
| A portion differs on a majority of the five factors | Account for it separately | 922-360-25-3 |
| Cost is not directly traceable to a separately accounted portion | Do not charge it there | 922-360-25-4 |
| Cost is subscriber-related or G&A | Expense as a period cost | 922-360-25-5 |
| Cost is initial installation | Capitalise, depreciate ≤ plant life | 922-360-25-7 |
| Cost is programming that benefits the completed system | Split current vs future; test each for recoverability | 922-360-35-1 |
| Computed capitalised interest exceeds actual interest | Cap at actual | 835-20 |
| Franchise application succeeds | Capitalise, amortise over franchise life | 922-350-25-3 |
| Franchise application fails or is abandoned | Expense immediately | 922-350-25-3 |

## Anti-patterns

- **Leaving the prematurity period open-ended.** It is presumed not to exceed two years and is set before first subscriber revenue.
- **Re-setting the period when the build slips.** Change requires highly unusual circumstances; schedule slippage is not one.
- **Capitalising subscriber acquisition costs.** Direct selling costs are subscriber-related period costs, not plant.
- **Pushing indirect rent and facilities into direct selling costs.** They are excluded by definition.
- **Capitalising all programming cost during the build.** Only the portion allocable to future operations, and it must be tested separately for recoverability.
- **Depreciating installation costs over a longer life than the plant.** The installation life is capped by the plant life.
- **Capitalising imputed interest above actual interest incurred.** Hard ceiling.
- **Allocating system-wide costs to a separately accounted portion** that they cannot be directly traced to.

## Key terms

- **Cable television plant** — the equipment needed to serve subscribers: **head-end** (receiving equipment for distant stations plus any origination studio), **cable** (cable and signal amplifiers, aerial or underground), **drops** (hardware providing access to the main cable, the run to the subscriber's set, and traps blocking particular channels), and **converters and descramblers** (attached where more than twelve channels or special services such as pay cable or two-way communication are provided).
- **Prematurity period** — the window in which the system is partly under construction and partly in service. Construction is not complete until head-end, main cable, and distribution cables are installed, plus reasonable time for subscriber drops.
- **Energising the system** — providing the signal for the first time.
- **Direct selling costs** — acquisition-focused selling costs, excluding supervisory, administrative, and indirect costs.
- **Subscriber-related costs** — all costs of obtaining and retaining subscribers.
