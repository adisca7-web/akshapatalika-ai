# ASC 960 / 962 / 965 — Plan Accounting

> Industry skill distilled from *Wiley GAAP 2020*, ch. 64 (Specialized Industry GAAP).
> Rules and structure synthesised locally; no source text reproduced.

**Three standards, three plan types**, all reporting on assets **segregated from the plan sponsor** and used to provide benefits under a plan agreement:

| Standard | Plan type |
|---|---|
| **ASC 960** | Defined benefit pension plans — the principal standard |
| **ASC 962** | Defined contribution pension plans |
| **ASC 965** | Health and welfare benefit plans |

**Out of scope**: plans of state and local governmental entities (see governmental GAAP). **Also out of scope**: the *employer's* accounting for its plans — that is ASC 710–718.

**ASC 960 applies only to ongoing plans**, not to plans terminated or expected to be terminated.

---

## Core model

Plan financial statements answer **one question**: can the plan pay benefits when they come due? That objective produces two structural departures from ordinary GAAP.

**1. The benefit obligation is not a liability.** Net assets are the residual after deducting liabilities, and **plan liabilities exclude accumulated plan benefits (defined benefit pension) and benefit obligations (defined benefit health and welfare)**. The obligation is instead presented as a separate statement, on the face of another statement, or in the notes — never as a balance sheet liability. The reader compares net assets *against* the obligation rather than netting them.

**2. Almost everything is at fair value.** Because participants' security depends on realisable asset values, investments are fair-valued, with tightly drawn exceptions for insurance and fully benefit-responsive contracts.

### Complete set of financial statements
- **Statement of net assets available for benefits** at plan year end (balance sheet equivalent)
- **Statement of changes in net assets available for benefits** for the plan year (income statement equivalent)
- **Defined benefit plans additionally**: the **actuarial present value of accumulated plan benefits** and the **changes** in it — as extra statements **or** in the notes (960-205-45-1, 962-205-45-1, 965-205-45-1)

**Cash flow statement**: defined benefit pension plans are **exempt** (ASC 230-10-15-4), as are other plans reporting similarly, including reporting investments at fair value. Presentation is **encouraged though not required** where it would be useful — notably where the plan **holds illiquid investments or buys investments with borrowed funds**.

## Subskills

### 1. Classify the plan
- **Single employer** (one employer or a controlled group) vs **multiemployer** (many related employers, often under a collective-bargaining agreement).
- **Contributory** (employer and participants fund) vs **noncontributory** (participants fund nothing).
- **Insured** (funded through insurance contracts), **self-funded** (contributions and investment return), or **split-funded** (a combination).

### 2. Measure investments
Most plan investments are at **fair value**: quoted market prices where an active market exists; otherwise valued **"in good faith" by the plan's trustees and administrator**, using the selling price of similar investments or discounted cash flows, with an independent expert where necessary. Financial statements must **identify investments by type and indicate how fair value was determined**.

The **contract value exceptions** are narrow:

| Contract | Measurement |
|---|---|
| Investment contracts, incl. **guaranteed investment contracts** issued by insurers, in a **defined benefit pension plan** | **Fair value** (960-325-35-1) |
| **Insurance contracts** — those incorporating **mortality or morbidity risk** (DB pension) | May be **contract value** |
| **Fully benefit-responsive** investment contracts (defined contribution and health and welfare plans) | **Contract value** — the only measure required by ASC 962 and ASC 965 |

Contract value is permitted **only if the plan also reports at contract value in its annual report filed with government agencies.**

A contract is **fully benefit-responsive** if: it is between the fund and the issuer and prohibits assignment or sale without issuer permission; the issuer is obligated to repay principal and interest, or a financially responsible third party assures the interest rate will not fall below zero; **all permitted participant-initiated transactions occur at contract value**; and an event limiting the fund's ability to transact at contract value **is not probable**.

**Alternative measurement date expedient** (960-325-35-4): where the fiscal period does not coincide with a month-end, plan assets may be measured at the **nearest month-end**, applied **consistently year to year**. Electing it requires disclosure of contributions, distributions, or significant events between that date and fiscal year-end, plus the use of the expedient, the alternative date, and the date used for investment-related accounts.

**Original cost** of investments is **neither required nor prohibited** in the basic financial statements (960-325-50-3, 962-325-50-2).

### 3. Other statement of net assets items
- **Contributions receivable**: amounts due at the reporting date from participants, employers, **withdrawing employers of a multiemployer plan**, and other sources such as a government grant or subsidy. They arise from formal employer commitments, legal requirements, or contractual requirements, and are **reduced by an allowance for uncollectible amounts** if warranted.
- **Long-lived assets used in plan operations** (buildings, equipment, furniture and fixtures, leasehold improvements) are at **cost less accumulated depreciation or amortisation** — not fair value.
- **Participant loans** in a defined contribution pension plan are classified as **notes receivable**, reported **separately from plan investments**, and measured at **unpaid principal plus accrued but unpaid interest** (962-310-45-2). Classifying them as notes receivable rather than investments is precisely what removes them from the fair value requirement.

### 4. Statement of changes in net assets
A comprehensive income statement of all recognised transactions and events changing net assets available for benefits, with separate amounts at minimum for:
- Contributions from **employers — separating cash from noncash**, from **participants** (including those transmitted by the sponsor), and from **other identified sources**
- **Net appreciation or depreciation in fair value for each significant class of investment** (realised and unrealised may be combined), presented separately for investments measured by **quoted market prices** and by **other means**
- **Investment income**, excluding that appreciation or depreciation
- **Benefits paid to participants**, excluding amounts paid by insurance contracts not included in plan assets
- **Payments to insurance companies** to purchase contracts excluded from plan assets
- **Administrative expenses**
- **Other changes**, with description

### 5. Master trust interests
Report an interest in a master trust as a **separate item** on the statement of net assets, and the **change in its value on a separate line** in the statement of changes. Disclose the master trust's investments and other assets and liabilities, the dollar amount of the plan's interest in those balances, and investments measured at fair value **by general type**. Also disclose **net appreciation or depreciation** and **investment income** for the master trust investments, the **basis used to allocate net assets and total investment income**, and, for plans with an individual interest, the **percentage interest** in the master trust.

### 6. Defined benefit specifics (ASC 960)
**Accumulated plan benefits** are future benefit payments attributable to employee service rendered to the benefit information date, covering retired or terminated employees and their beneficiaries, beneficiaries of deceased employees, and present employees and their beneficiaries.

**Plan provisions govern** the measurement wherever possible; where benefits earned each year are not determinable from those provisions, ASC 960 supplies a measurement formula.

Critically, the measurement **assumes an ongoing plan** — the analogue of the going concern assumption. Therefore discount rates are based on **rates of return on investments for the benefit deferral period**, and **employee turnover and mortality** are considered.

Where the plan has a postretirement **medical benefit component funded under IRC §401(h)**, ASC 960-30-45-4 through 45-10 give specialised presentation rules, necessary because of the legal requirement to account for and fund those arrangements separately.

### 7. Defined contribution specifics (ASC 962)
**Three general types**: **profit-sharing plans** (neither a pension plan as defined in the IRC nor a stock bonus plan; employer contributions discretionary or by fixed formula, and **need not come from profits** despite the name), **money purchase pension plans** (contributions on a fixed formula **unrelated to profits**), and **stock bonus plans** (distributions in employer stock unless the participant elects otherwise). Within these sit 401(k), 403(b), savings, ESOP, target benefit, and Keogh plans.

**Three distinguishing attributes** versus defined benefit:
1. Employer contributions are **discretionary or by contractual formula**, not actuarially determined.
2. **Individual accounts** are maintained per participant, not a single pooled account.
3. Benefits are **whatever has accumulated** in the participant's account at retirement or withdrawal, not defined in the plan agreement. If vested, the account value is paid out or used to buy an annuity.

**Liabilities** of a defined contribution plan — refunds of excess contributions, income taxes payable by the plan, and other expenses — are **deducted to arrive at net assets available for benefits** (962-205-45-4).

### 8. Health and welfare specifics (ASC 965)
These plans stipulate a **determinable benefit amount**, paid to the participant or to a third party on their behalf, at retirement or during the postemployment period between termination and retirement. Benefit levels turn on **length of employment, age, and salary**. Employer contributions may be **actuarially determined, based on actual claims paid, or by a sponsor formula** — and **the funding method is irrelevant**: if the purpose is to provide a defined benefit, it is a defined benefit health and welfare plan. ASC 965 requires such plans to use certain **ASC 715** provisions to measure benefit obligations.

**Recognise a benefit obligation for postemployment benefits for current participants if all four hold** (965-30-25-3):
1. Participants' rights are attributable to **services already rendered**.
2. Benefits **vest or accumulate**.
3. Payment is **probable**.
4. The amount can be **reasonably estimated**.

If conditions 1 and 2 are **not** met, recognise the obligation if the **event giving rise to the liability has already occurred** and the amount can be reasonably estimated (965-30-25-4).

**Measured at plan year end**, the benefit obligation is the actuarial present value of: claims payable; **claims incurred but not reported (IBNR)**; insurance premiums due; accumulated eligibility credits and postemployment benefits net of amounts currently payable; and postretirement benefits for retired participants and beneficiaries, other eligible participants, and **participants not yet eligible** (965-50-35-1).

A **reconciliation** of the change in benefit obligation over the year must identify significant factors, classified into **at least three categories** (965-30-45-5):
1. **Amounts currently payable** — claims payable, claims IBNR, premiums due insurers
2. **Accumulated eligibility credits and postemployment benefits**, net of amounts currently payable
3. **Postretirement benefit obligations**, net of amounts currently payable and claims IBNR

Each category shows at minimum the effects of plan amendments; changes in the nature of the plan such as a merger or spin-off; changes in actuarial assumptions; benefits accumulated during the year; benefits paid; the **interest component** from amortising the discount; and other changes. **The last four may be combined into a single "other changes" line.**

**Defined contribution health and welfare plans do not report benefit obligation information**, because the plan's obligation is limited to the amounts accumulated in participants' accounts. They must, however, disclose the **accounting policy and disposition of forfeited nonvested accounts**.

### 9. Risks, regulation, and termination
**Risks and uncertainties** (ASC 275) unique to plans: a significant industry downturn prompting early retirement to avoid layoff — especially where participants are concentrated in one industry or employer; the likelihood an employer **significantly increases benefits to avoid a union walkout**; a planned downsizing expected to offer early retirement; and **investments in the stock of the employer**.

**ERISA** sets minimum standards for participation, vesting, and funding, defines fiduciary responsibilities and conduct standards, and requires annual summarised reporting to participants. The **DOL** and **IRS** issue reporting and disclosure regulations; plans report annually to the DOL, IRS, and, where applicable, the **PBGC**, for many plans via **Form 5500**, which includes GAAP financial statements plus supplementary schedules.

**IRC §401(a) qualification** brings favourable treatment: the sponsor deducts contributions currently, participants are not taxed on contributions or accumulated earnings until distribution, distributions may receive favourable treatment, and the plan is **exempt from income taxes except on unrelated business income**. **Nonqualified plans**, which generally benefit only a few key employees selectively, receive none of this.

**Terminating plans** (960-40-20): if **liquidation is imminent before the end of the plan year**, the plan is a terminating plan — **even if another plan will replace it**. It may continue operating as long as necessary to pay accrued benefits. **Prominent disclosure** of the circumstances is required in all financial statements issued after the decision to terminate. Financial statements are prepared on the **liquidation basis** for plan years ending after the determination that termination is imminent, and the liquidation basis for accumulated plan benefits or benefit obligations **may differ from the ongoing-plan actuarial present value** — for example, some or all benefits may **become vested on termination**.

## Decision rules

| If | Then | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Presenting accumulated plan benefits / benefit obligations | **Not a liability** — separate statement, face of another, or notes | 960/965 |
| Plan is a defined benefit pension plan | **Cash flow statement exempt** | 230-10-15-4 |
| Plan holds illiquid investments or buys with borrowed funds | Cash flow statement **encouraged** | 960-205-45-6 |
| Contract incorporates mortality or morbidity risk (DB pension) | May use **contract value** | 960-325-35-1 |
| Contract is a GIC without mortality/morbidity risk (DB pension) | **Fair value** | 960-325-35-1 |
| Contract is fully benefit-responsive (DC / H&W) | **Contract value** | 962 / 965 |
| Plan does not report contract value to government agencies | **Contract value not permitted** | 960-325-35 |
| Fiscal period does not end at month-end | May use **nearest month-end**, consistently, with disclosure | 960-325-35-4 |
| Loans to DC plan participants | **Notes receivable** at unpaid principal plus accrued interest — not fair value | 962-310-45-2 |
| Long-lived assets used in operations | **Cost less depreciation** | — |
| H&W benefit attributable to past service, vests/accumulates, probable, estimable | **Recognise the obligation** | 965-30-25-3 |
| Rights not attributable to past service and do not vest | Recognise only if the triggering **event has occurred** and it is estimable | 965-30-25-4 |
| Plan is defined contribution health and welfare | **No benefit obligation reporting**; disclose forfeiture policy | 965-205-50-1 |
| Liquidation imminent before plan year end | **Terminating plan** — liquidation basis, prominent disclosure | 960-40-20 |
| A replacement plan will succeed the terminated plan | Still a terminating plan | 960-40-20 |

## Anti-patterns

- **Presenting accumulated plan benefits as a liability** on the statement of net assets. Net assets are computed before them.
- **Reporting participant loans at fair value.** They are notes receivable at unpaid principal plus accrued interest.
- **Using contract value for a GIC in a defined benefit pension plan.** Only contracts with mortality or morbidity risk qualify, and only if contract value is also used in the government filing.
- **Continuing fully benefit-responsive treatment** when an event limiting contract-value transactions becomes probable.
- **Measuring accumulated plan benefits on a termination basis** for an ongoing plan. The ongoing-plan assumption drives the discount rate, turnover, and mortality.
- **Omitting the cash flow statement** where the plan holds illiquid investments or leverages purchases — permitted, but the disclosure is expected to be useful there.
- **Combining employer cash and noncash contributions** in the statement of changes.
- **Netting a master trust interest into general investments** instead of presenting it separately with its own change line.
- **Treating a plan as ongoing** once liquidation is imminent, or assuming a replacement plan avoids terminating-plan accounting.
- **Applying ASC 960/962/965 to the employer's own books.** That is ASC 710–718.

## Key terms

- **Accumulated plan benefits** — future benefit payments attributable to service rendered to the benefit information date.
- **Net assets** — residual interest in plan assets after liabilities, **excluding** accumulated plan benefits or benefit obligations.
- **Noncontributory plan** — participants make no contributions.
- **Health and welfare benefit plan** — provides medical, dental, visual or other health care, insurance, disability, vacation, education, or dependent care.
- **Liquidation** — converting assets to cash and settling obligations in anticipation of ceasing all activities. **Dissolution by acquisition or merger with the expectation of continuing the business is not liquidation.**
- **Multiemployer plan** — covers employees of many related employers, often under a collective-bargaining agreement.
- **IBNR** — claims incurred but not reported.
