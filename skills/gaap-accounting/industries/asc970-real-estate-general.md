# ASC 970 — Real Estate: General

> Industry skill distilled from *Wiley GAAP 2020*, ch. 64 (Specialized Industry GAAP).
> Rules and structure synthesised locally; no source text reproduced.

**Governs the accounting for costs of acquiring and developing real estate projects**, whether the real estate is ultimately **rented or sold**.

**Does not apply to** (ASC 970-10-15-8, 15-9):
- Real estate developed by an entity **for its own use** rather than for sale or rental
- **Initial direct costs of leases** (ASC 842)
- Costs directly related to **manufacturing, merchandising, or service** activities rather than real estate activities
- Rental operations in which the **predominant rental period is less than a month**

---

## Core model

Real estate development is a long capitalisation exercise punctuated by three questions: **when may I start capitalising**, **how do I spread pooled costs over units**, and **when must I stop**.

The answers are unusually mechanical, and each has a bright line:

- **Start**: preacquisition costs capitalise only once acquisition is **probable**.
- **Stop**: taxes and insurance stop capitalising when the property is **substantially complete and ready for its intended use**; rental cost amortisation starts when it is **substantially completed and available for occupancy** — defined as tenant improvements completed, **or one year from the end of major construction activity**, whichever comes first.
- **Spread**: allocate by specific identification; failing that, **pre-construction costs by relative fair value of each parcel** and **construction costs by relative sales value of each unit**.

## Subskills

### 1. Preacquisition costs
Capitalise if the payment relates to **an option** to obtain the property, **or** if **all three** hold (ASC 970-340-25-3):
1. Costs are **directly identified** with the property.
2. Costs **would be capitalised if the property were already acquired**.
3. Acquisition of the option or property is **probable** — the purchaser wants the property, believes it is available, and has the **ability to finance** it.

Once capitalised they become project costs. If they are **not recoverable in the future, or the property is not acquired, recognise them as expense** (970-340-25-4).

### 2. Taxes, insurance, and project costs
**Real estate taxes and insurance** are capitalised as property costs **only while the property is undergoing activities necessary to get it ready for its intended use**. Once substantially complete and ready, expense them (970-340-25-8).

**Project costs** recognised as an asset under ASC 340-40-25-1 through 25-8 are capitalised as a cost of the project (970-360-25-2). **Indirect costs relating to several projects** are capitalised and **allocated** among them (970-360-25-3). **Overhead that does not clearly relate to any project — general and administrative expense — is expensed as incurred.**

### 3. Amenities
Amenities are features enhancing attractiveness or perceived value: golf courses, utility plants, clubhouses, pools, tennis courts, indoor recreation, parking. Their treatment depends on their destination (ASC 970-340-25-9 to 25-11):

| Amenity destination | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Sold or transferred with individual units** | Costs **in excess of anticipated proceeds** are treated as **common costs** of the project |
| **Sold separately or retained by the developer** | Capitalise; costs **in excess of estimated fair value** are treated as common costs |

For the second case, **fair value is determined as of the expected date of substantial physical completion, and the amount allocated to the amenity is not revised later.** On a later sale, gain or loss arises where the selling price differs from that **fair value less accumulated depreciation**.

Amenity costs are allocated among **land parcels benefited for which development is probable**. A parcel's fair value is affected by its physical characteristics, its **highest and best use**, and the time and cost required to achieve that use.

**Operating results of an amenity**: before completion and availability for use, operating income or loss is an **adjustment to common costs**; after that date it goes to the **income statement**.

### 4. Incidental operations
Incidental operations are revenue-producing activities during the **holding or development period** undertaken to **reduce the cost of developing** the property — as distinguished from activities designed to generate a profit or return from its use.

Net incremental revenue against incremental costs (ASC 970-340-25-12):
- **Excess of incremental revenue over incremental costs → reduces capitalised project costs.**
- **Excess of costs over revenues → expensed as incurred.**

**Incremental** is the operative word: interest, taxes, insurance, security and similar costs that would be incurred **regardless** of whether incidental operations were conducted are **not** incremental costs.

### 5. Allocate and revise
Allocation hierarchy (ASC 970-360-30-1):
1. **Specific identification.**
2. If not feasible: pre-construction costs by **relative fair value of each parcel before construction**; construction costs by **relative sales value of each unit**.
3. If estimating relative values is impractical: **square footage or another area method**, or other appropriate methods.

Review estimates and cost allocations **at the end of each period until the project is substantially complete and available for sale**, revising and reallocating for material changes (970-340-35-1).

### 6. Abandonment and change in use
- **Abandonment**: expense **all** capitalised costs. They may **not be reallocated** to other components of the project or to other projects (970-360-40-1). This is the strictest rule in the topic — the temptation to shift a failed phase's cost onto a successful one is closed off explicitly.
- **Real estate dedicated to governmental units is not deemed abandoned**; its costs become **project common costs** (970-360-35-1).
- **Change in use**: charge to expense the costs incurred and expected to be incurred that **exceed the estimated value of the revised project** when substantially complete and ready for intended use (970-360-35-2). **If no formal plan exists**, expense project costs in excess of **current net realisable value**.

### 7. Selling and rental costs
**Selling costs** are evaluated for capitalisation under **ASC 340-40-25-1 through 25-8**.

**Rental costs** incurred and **reasonably expected to be recoverable from future rental operations** are capitalised, **excluding** initial direct costs as defined for leases; non-qualifying costs are expensed as incurred (970-340-25-13).

Amortise capitalised rental costs (970-340-35-2):
- Over the **lease term** if directly related to a specific operating or direct financing lease, otherwise over the **period of expected benefit**.
- **Amortisation begins when the project is substantially completed and available for occupancy.**
- **Estimated unrecoverable amounts are expensed when it is probable the lease will be terminated.**

**Substantially completed and available for occupancy** means tenant improvements are completed **or one year has passed from the end of major construction activity** (970-340-25-18). Thereafter normal operations apply, with all revenues and costs — including depreciation and amortised costs — in the income statement. **If part of a project is occupied while other parts are incomplete, the completed portions are treated as separate projects.**

### 8. Tax Increment Financing Entities and special assessments
TIFEs are special taxing districts formed to finance and operate **municipality-owned infrastructure** — roads, water mains, electric lines, sewers — used to revitalise a discrete area and facilitate adjoining private development. The TIFE or municipality issues bonds, often **tax-exempt under IRC §141**, repaid from special assessments such as user fees, tolls, sales taxes, real estate taxes, and hotel bed taxes. Assessments also fund ongoing infrastructure repairs and maintenance. Terms are often **jointly negotiated with developers as an inducement to invest**.

Depending on the statute, ordinance, bond indenture, and other legal documents, the property owner or developer may be required to record:
- **A liability for a special assessment** where the assessment is levied on each individual property owner at an amount that is **fixed or determinable** and covers a **determinable period of time** (ASC 970-470).
- **A guarantee liability under ASC 450** where the developer has, for example, contractually agreed to **cover shortfalls in required annual debt service**, **pledged company assets as collateral**, or **provided a letter of credit or other credit enhancement** (ASC 970-470-25-1).

## Decision rules

| If | Then | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-acquisition spending, acquisition **probable** and other tests met | Capitalise | 970-340-25-3 |
| Acquisition does not occur, or costs become unrecoverable | Expense | 970-340-25-4 |
| Property is being readied for intended use | Capitalise taxes and insurance | 970-340-25-8 |
| Property substantially complete and ready | **Stop** capitalising taxes and insurance | 970-340-25-8 |
| Overhead does not clearly relate to any project | Expense as G&A | 970-360-25-3 |
| Amenity sold with units, cost > anticipated proceeds | Excess is a **common cost** | 970-340-25-9 |
| Amenity retained or sold separately, cost > fair value | Excess is a common cost; **do not revise** the allocated amount later | 970-340-25-9 |
| Incidental operations produce net incremental revenue | **Reduce capitalised project cost** | 970-340-25-12 |
| Incidental operations run at a net cost | Expense as incurred | 970-340-25-12 |
| Cost would be incurred anyway (interest, taxes, security) | **Not incremental** — exclude from the netting | 970-340 |
| Allocating pre-construction cost | Relative **fair value** of parcels | 970-360-30-1 |
| Allocating construction cost | Relative **sales value** of units | 970-360-30-1 |
| Project abandoned | Expense all capitalised cost; **no reallocation** | 970-360-40-1 |
| Real estate dedicated to a governmental unit | Not abandonment — treat as common cost | 970-360-35-1 |
| Use changes and costs exceed the revised project's value | Charge the excess to expense | 970-360-35-2 |
| No formal plan for the project | Expense costs above current **NRV** | 970-360-35-2 |
| Rental cost reasonably recoverable from future rentals | Capitalise (excluding lease initial direct costs) | 970-340-25-13 |
| Tenant improvements done, or 1 year past major construction | Project is available for occupancy — start amortising | 970-340-25-18 |
| Part of a project occupied, rest incomplete | Treat completed portions as **separate projects** | 970-340-25-18 |
| Lease termination becomes probable | Expense estimated unrecoverable amounts | 970-340-35-2 |
| Special assessment fixed/determinable over a determinable period | Record a liability | 970-470 |
| Developer guarantees TIFE debt service or pledges collateral | Record a **guarantee liability** under ASC 450 | 970-470-25-1 |

## Anti-patterns

- **Capitalising preacquisition costs before acquisition is probable.**
- **Continuing to capitalise taxes and insurance** after the property is ready for its intended use.
- **Reallocating an abandoned project's costs** to surviving phases or other projects. Explicitly prohibited.
- **Revising the fair value allocated to a retained amenity** after substantial physical completion. It is fixed at that date.
- **Treating interest, taxes, insurance, or security as incremental costs** of incidental operations.
- **Recognising incidental operations profit as income.** It reduces capitalised project cost.
- **Waiting indefinitely to start amortising rental costs.** The one-year-after-major-construction test applies even if tenant improvements are unfinished.
- **Treating a partly occupied project as a single project.** Completed portions are separate.
- **Overlooking a TIFE guarantee.** Debt service shortfall guarantees, pledged collateral, and letters of credit create ASC 450 liabilities.

## Key terms

- **Common costs** — costs relating to two or more units within a project.
- **Project costs** — costs clearly associated with acquisition, development, and construction.
- **Indirect project costs** — post-acquisition costs such as construction administration, legal fees, and office costs clearly relating to projects under development.
- **Preacquisition costs** — costs incurred for the express purpose of, but prior to, obtaining a property (surveying, zoning or traffic studies, option payments).
- **Incidental operations** — revenue-producing activities during holding or development undertaken to **reduce development cost**, not to generate a return.
- **Incremental costs / revenue of incidental operations** — those that would not be incurred or earned but for the incidental operations.
- **Relative fair value before construction** — each parcel's fair value relative to others, **excluding** value added by on-site development and construction.
- **Phase** — a contractually or physically distinguishable portion of a project.
- **Amenities** — features enhancing attractiveness or perceived value.
