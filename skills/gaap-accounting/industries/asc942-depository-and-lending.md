# ASC 942 — Financial Services: Depository and Lending

> Industry skill distilled from *Wiley GAAP 2020*, ch. 64 (Specialized Industry GAAP).
> Rules and structure synthesised locally; no source text reproduced.

**Applies to** banks, thrifts, credit unions, corporate credit unions, finance companies, and mortgage companies (ASC 942-10-15-2, 15-3) — **and to the financing activities of manufacturers, retailers, wholesalers, and other business enterprises**. That last clause matters: a captive finance arm inside an industrial group is inside this topic.

---

## Core model

ASC 942 is largely **incremental and presentational**. The heavy lifting sits in general GAAP — ASC 310 (receivables), ASC 326 (credit losses), ASC 320 (securities), ASC 860 (transfers), ASC 815 (derivatives). What ASC 942 adds is the set of rules that only make sense for a regulated deposit-taker: how to treat regulator-specific assets, what capital disclosures are required, and where the normal presentation rules are relaxed because transaction volumes are enormous.

The recurring theme is **regulatory capital as the primary user concern**. Depositors and regulators care whether the institution can absorb losses, so the disclosure package is far heavier than the recognition guidance.

## Subskills

### 1. Receivables and troubled loans
- Where loans to financially troubled countries meet the ASC 326-20 credit loss conditions, establish **loan loss allowances by charges to income** (942-310-35-1).
- **Payments received while interest accrual is suspended**: determine whether the payment reduces principal or is interest income. **If ultimate collectibility of the whole or part is in doubt, apply the payment to reduce principal to the extent it reduces that doubt** (942-310-35-2).
- Once a country **normalises relations** with the international financial community and becomes current, the creditor may recognise interest receipts as income — **assuming the loss allowance is adequate** (942-310-35-3).
- Provide for uncollectible amounts on **customers' acceptance liabilities** where necessary (942-310-35-8).

### 2. Debt-equity swaps
A debt-equity swap exchanges a **monetary asset for a nonmonetary asset**. The accounting is deliberately asymmetric:

| Item | Treatment | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Fees and transaction costs | **Expense as incurred** | 942-310-25-2 |
| Fair value of equity or net assets **below** recorded investment in the loan | Recognise a **loss as of the date the transaction is agreed to**, generally **charged to the allowance for loan losses** | 942-310-35-5 |
| Loss recoveries or **gains** | **Not recorded until realised in unrestricted cash** | 942-310-35-6 |

Losses are recognised on agreement; gains wait for cash. That asymmetry is the point.

### 3. Investments and restricted stock
- **Accretion for debt securities** runs from **purchase date to maturity date**, unless other guidance applies (942-320-35-1).
- **Federal Home Loan Bank and Federal Reserve Bank stock** is classified as a **restricted investment security, carried at cost** and evaluated for impairment (942-325-25-1, 35-1). Impairment is assessed on **recoverability of par value**, and **temporary declines are not recognised** (942-325-35-3). Cash and stock dividends on FHLB stock are **reported as income** (942-325-35-2).
- **Regular-way** purchases and sales of securities are recorded on the **trade date** (942-325-25-2).

### 4. Deposit insurance — the credit union exception
A genuine divergence driven by how the funds legally operate:

| Institution | Deposit insurance payment |
|---|---|
| **Credit unions** (NCUSIF) | Reported as an **asset** if the amounts are **fully refundable** (942-325-25-3) |
| **Banks and thrifts** | **Expensed as incurred** |

Credit unions must review refundability of Share Insurance Fund deposits and premiums **for impairment**, considering the financial condition of both the credit union and the fund (942-325-35-4a). If NCUSIF equity exceeds normal operating levels the Administrator must **distribute** the excess to credit unions (35-4b). Where the NCUSIF **waives premiums**, industry practice is to **net imputed earnings on the deposits against imputed premium expense** rather than presenting them gross (35-4c).

### 5. Liabilities
- **Short sales**: obligations are reported as liabilities, usually captioned **"securities sold, not yet purchased"** (942-405-25-1).
- **Deposits**: the liability is recognised **when deposits are received**, and the **deposit float is recorded both as an asset and a liability** (942-405-25-3).
- **Credit unions**: savings accounts must be **unequivocally listed as a liability**, presented either as the **first item** in the liabilities and equity section or as a **separate subtotal before total liabilities** (942-405-55-1, 55-2).
- **FICO assessment**: report as a **period cost when incurred** (942-720-25-1).

### 6. Statement of cash flows relief
Banks, savings institutions, and credit unions **need not report gross** cash receipts and payments for (942-230-45-1): deposits placed with other financial institutions, withdrawals of deposits, time deposits accepted, repayments of deposits, loans made to customers, and principal collections of loans.

Two important limits:
- **They are not exempt from presenting a cash flow statement** — only from grossing up these high-volume categories.
- Where the enterprise is part of a consolidated group, its **net** receipts and payments are reported **separately from the gross** amounts of the consolidated enterprise.

**Principal payments received under sales-type and direct financing leases are classified as investing activities** (942-230-45-4).

### 7. Regulatory capital disclosures
The largest single requirement in the topic. Banks and savings institutions must disclose, at minimum (942-505-50-1):

a. A description of regulatory capital requirements for **capital adequacy** and for the **prompt corrective action** provisions of **Section 38 of the Federal Deposit Insurance Act**.
b. The actual or possible **material effects of noncompliance**.
c. **Whether the entity is in compliance**, including required and actual ratios and amounts of **Tier 1 leverage** (which may include Common Equity Tier 1 leverage), **Tier 1 risk-based**, **total risk-based** capital, **tangible capital** (savings institutions), and **Tier 3** capital for market risk (certain banks and holding companies) — plus factors significantly affecting capital adequacy such as volatile capital components, qualitative factors, and regulatory mandates.
d. The **prompt corrective action category** as of each balance sheet date presented, per the most recent notification.
e. Whether management believes any conditions or events **since notification** have changed the category.

Additional layers:
- **State-imposed requirements** more stringent than or significantly different from federal ones must also be disclosed (50-1A, and 50-2 for credit unions).
- **Adequately capitalized or undercapitalized** entities present the minimum amounts and ratios required to be categorised as adequately capitalized, **including the effect of any supervisory action imposed**. Narrative or tabular form is acceptable (50-1B).
- **Leverage ratio benchmark**: entities with **CAMELS ratings of 1**, not anticipating or experiencing significant growth and with well-diversified risk, must maintain a minimum leverage ratio of **3.0%**. **All others require an additional 100 to 200 basis points.** Institution-specific requirements above statutory minimums must be disclosed and form the basis for the compliance assertion.
- **Capital category effective date** (50-1C): the most recent of the date the institution filed a regulatory financial report, the date a final regulatory examination report was delivered, or the date the primary regulator gave written notice of the category or a change in it.
- Where the entity is **not in compliance** or is **less than adequately capitalized**, disclose the possible material effects on the financial statements (50-1D, 50-1E). Other regulatory limitations that could materially affect economic resources must likewise be disclosed (50-1F).

**Holding companies** (50-1G): present these disclosures for **all significant subsidiaries**; bank holding companies also present them for themselves **except item (d)**. Savings institution holding companies are **not subject to separate regulatory capital requirements** from their subsidiaries, and bank holding companies are **not subject to the prompt corrective action provisions**.

**Credit unions** (50-1H) disclose a parallel package: regulatory requirements for capital adequacy and prompt corrective action, effects of noncompliance, whether in compliance, the PCA category, effects if not in compliance as of the most recent balance sheet date or issuance date, and whether post-balance-sheet events have changed the PCA category.

**Branches of foreign institutions** (50-3, 50-4): disclose requirements to maintain **capital-equivalent deposits** and, depending on circumstances, **supervisory-mandated reserves**, with **quantitative** disclosure of mandated requirements and actual balances. If an uncertainty about the **parent** creates higher-than-normal risk to the branch's viability, disclose it; absent such factors, parent capital disclosures are not required.

**Trust operations**: where capital requirements are based on trust assets under management, disclose their existence, the ramifications of failure, and the entity's position relative to them.

### 8. Other presentation and disclosure
- **Cash restrictions** (942-210-50-1): disclose restrictions on use or availability of cash — for example deposits with a Federal Reserve Bank, Federal Home Loan Bank, or correspondent institutions to meet reserve requirements, and deposits under **formal compensating balance agreements**.
- **Debt** (942-470-45-1, 15-2, 50-3): present significant categories of borrowings as **separate line items**. **Transfers of mortgages accounted for under ASC 860 are classified as debt**, separately from advances, other notes payable, and subordinated debt. Disclose title/nature, interest rate, payment terms, maturity, conversion and redemption features, seniority, and restrictive covenants.
- **Off-balance-sheet credit risk** (942-825-50-1), except instruments within ASC 815: disclose face or contract amount; nature and terms including credit and market risk, cash requirements, and the related accounting policy under ASC 235-10; the collateral policy; access to collateral; and a description of the collateral.
- **Deferred taxes** (942-740-50-1): where a deferred tax liability is **not recognised** because of exceptions relating to a savings and loan's **bad-debt reserve**, disclose the types of temporary differences, the events that would make them taxable, the cumulative amount of each type, and the unrecognised deferred tax liability.
- **Trust-preferred arrangements** (942-810-45-1): where a bank does **not** consolidate the trust, report its **debt issued to the trust** and its **equity-method investment in the trust's common stock**.
- Consult **SEC Regulation S-X** Rules 9-02 (general), 9-03 (balance sheet), and 9-04 (income statement).

## Decision rules

| If | Then | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Payment received while accrual is suspended and collectibility is doubtful | **Reduce principal** to the extent doubt is reduced | 942-310-35-2 |
| Debt-equity swap fair value below recorded investment | Loss **on the agreement date**, to the allowance | 942-310-35-5 |
| Debt-equity swap produces a gain | **Wait for unrestricted cash** | 942-310-35-6 |
| Holding FHLB or Federal Reserve Bank stock | Restricted security **at cost**; impair on **par value recoverability**; ignore temporary declines | 942-325-25-1, 35-3 |
| Credit union pays into NCUSIF and amounts are fully refundable | **Asset** | 942-325-25-3 |
| Bank or thrift pays deposit insurance | **Expense** | 942-325-25-3 |
| Recording a regular-way security trade | Trade date | 942-325-25-2 |
| Short sale executed | Liability: "securities sold, not yet purchased" | 942-405-25-1 |
| Credit union presenting savings accounts | First item in liabilities and equity, or a separate subtotal before total liabilities | 942-405-55-2 |
| Reporting deposit and loan cash flows | **Net** presentation permitted; a cash flow statement is **still required** | 942-230-45-1 |
| Principal received on sales-type / direct financing leases | **Investing** activities | 942-230-45-4 |
| Mortgage transfer accounted for under ASC 860 | Classify as **debt**, separate from advances and subordinated debt | 942-470-15-2 |
| CAMELS 1, no significant growth, diversified risk | Minimum leverage ratio **3.0%** | 942-505-50-1B |
| Any other institution | **+100 to 200 basis points** above that minimum | 942-505-50-1B |
| Bank does not consolidate a trust-preferred trust | Report the debt issued **and** the equity-method investment | 942-810-45-1 |

## Anti-patterns

- **Recognising a debt-equity swap gain before realisation in unrestricted cash**, while recognising losses only on settlement. The asymmetry runs the other way.
- **Writing down FHLB or Federal Reserve Bank stock for a temporary decline.** Only par-value recoverability matters.
- **Expensing NCUSIF deposits at a credit union** where they are fully refundable, or **capitalising** bank/thrift deposit insurance payments.
- **Omitting the cash flow statement** because deposits and loans may be reported net. Only grossing-up is relieved.
- **Reporting net cash flows inside consolidated gross amounts** without separate presentation.
- **Applying a payment to interest income** while collectibility of the loan is in doubt.
- **Burying trust-preferred debt** by neither consolidating the trust nor reporting the debt and equity-method investment.
- **Disclosing only statutory minimum capital ratios** where the regulator has advised higher institution-specific levels — those higher levels drive both the disclosure and the compliance assertion.
- **Presenting credit union savings accounts** anywhere other than first in liabilities and equity or as a separate subtotal.

## Key terms

- **Accretable yield** — cash flows expected to be collected in excess of the initial investment (loans acquired in transfers).
- **Nonaccretable difference** — the excess of contractual cash flows over expected cash flows.
- **Carrying amount** — face amount of the interest-bearing asset plus or minus unamortised premium or discount.
- **Origination fees** — fees charged to the borrower in originating, refinancing, or restructuring a loan: points, management, arrangement, placement, application, underwriting, plus syndication and participation fees to the extent associated with the **retained** portion.
- **Commitment fees** — fees for entering an agreement obligating the enterprise to make or acquire a loan or satisfy an obligation, **including letters of credit** and obligations to purchase loans or pass-through certificates.
- **Incremental direct costs** — costs that result directly from and are essential to the lending transaction and would not have been incurred but for it.
- **Net-spread method** — views the acquisition of a savings and loan association as acquiring a **leveraged whole** rather than separate assets and liabilities.
- **General reserve** — a portion of earnings regulators require savings and loans to set aside as depositor protection.
- **Taxable income** (in this context) — pretax accounting income adjusted for reversal of estimated loss provisions on loans and foreclosed property, gains/losses on sales of such property, and permanent differences, then after the IRC bad-debt deduction.
