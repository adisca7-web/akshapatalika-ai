# ASC 928 — Entertainment: Music

> Industry skill distilled from *Wiley GAAP 2020*, ch. 64 (Specialized Industry GAAP).
> Rules and structure synthesised locally; no source text reproduced.

**Applies to** the recording and music industry, where business is transacted through **licence agreements** — arrangements by which the owner of a music master or copyright (licensor) grants a licensee the right to sell or distribute recordings or sheet music, for a fixed fee or a fee based on sales. Licence agreements are modifications of the compulsory provisions of copyright law.

ASC 928 answers the same question from **both sides**, and the answers are deliberately asymmetric.

---

## Core model

The recurring pattern is **money paid before it is earned**, and the accounting turns entirely on *recoverability*.

- A **minimum guarantee** flows one way (licensee → licensor) and is a liability to the receiver, an asset to the payer.
- An **advance royalty** flows to the artist and is an asset only while the licensor still expects to recover it out of future royalties.

The moment expected recovery fails, the unrecoverable portion is expensed — in the period the loss becomes apparent, not spread forward.

---

## Subskills

### 1. Licensor — minimum guarantees received
Record the guarantee **first as a liability**, then amortise it into income as the licence fee is earned (ASC 928-405-25-1).

If the amount of the fee earned is **indeterminable**, recognise revenue **straight-line over the licence period**. Straight-line is the fallback when the earning pattern cannot be measured, not a free policy choice.

### 2. Licensor — artist compensation
**Royalties** are paid to producers, songwriters, and other artists for participation in recordings, and to music publishers for their copyright interest. Artist amounts come from personal service contracts, usually a percentage of sales activity and licence fee income **adjusted for estimated sales returns**; publishing royalties follow copyright law. Royalties are a **period expense** of the licensor.

**Advance royalties** are capitalised as assets **only if** the licensor expects the artist's recording to be successful enough to fully recover the advance from future royalties (ASC 928-340-35-1). Then:
- Charge the capitalised advance to expense as the artist earns royalties.
- **Apportion between current and noncurrent** according to how soon each portion is expected to be charged to expense.
- If it later appears the advance will **not** be fully recovered, expense the unrecoverable portion **in the period the loss becomes apparent**.

### 3. Licensor — cost to produce record masters
Producing a master costs musical talent (musicians, backing vocals, arrangements), technical talent (engineering, directing, mixing), recording equipment use, and studio facility charges. Under a standard artist contract the media company bears part of the cost and recovers part from the artist out of designated royalties — though either party may contractually agree to bear all or most.

Split the cost by who ultimately bears it:

| Portion | Treatment | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Borne by the media company | Asset **if** it expects sales sufficient for full recovery of accumulated costs; otherwise **expense** | 928-340-25-2 |
| Recoverable from the artist's royalties | Treat as **advance royalties** — follow the advance-royalty rules above | 928-340-25-3 |

Amounts capitalised are amortised over the **useful life of the recording, in a manner relating cost to estimated net revenue** to be recognised (ASC 928-340-35-2) — a forecast-style match, not straight-line.

### 4. Licensee — minimum guarantees paid
Record the advance payment as an **asset**, then amortise to expense according to the terms of the licence agreement. If the guarantee later appears not fully recoverable through use of the rights received, **expense the unrecoverable portion** (ASC 928-340-35-3).

### 5. Licensee — indeterminable fees
Some agreements stipulate fees whose amount is unknown until the agreement expires — for example a fee charged to a recording club for free recordings given away beyond a set number. The licensee must **estimate and accrue these on a licence-by-licence basis**, not in aggregate.

## Decision rules

| If | Then | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Licensor receives a minimum guarantee | Liability first; amortise to income as earned | 928-405-25-1 |
| Fee earned is indeterminable | Straight-line over the licence period | 928-405-25-1 |
| Licensor expects full recovery of an advance | Capitalise as an asset | 928-340-35-1 |
| Recovery of an advance becomes doubtful | Expense the unrecoverable portion when the loss becomes apparent | 928-340-35-1 |
| Master cost is recoverable from artist royalties | Account for it as an advance royalty | 928-340-25-3 |
| Master cost borne by the company, recovery not expected | Expense | 928-340-25-2 |
| Capitalised master cost | Amortise over the recording's useful life against estimated net revenue | 928-340-35-2 |
| Licensee pays a minimum guarantee | Asset; amortise per agreement terms | 928-340-35-3 |
| Fee amount unknown until expiry | Estimate and accrue licence-by-licence | — |

## Anti-patterns

- **Recognising a minimum guarantee as revenue on receipt.** It is a liability until the licence fee is earned.
- **Capitalising every advance royalty.** Capitalisation requires an expectation of full recovery; without it the advance is an expense.
- **Deferring recognition of an unrecoverable advance.** The loss lands in the period it becomes apparent.
- **Classifying the whole advance as noncurrent.** It is apportioned by expected timing of charge to expense.
- **Straight-lining master cost amortisation.** It follows estimated net revenue over the recording's useful life.
- **Forgetting the sales-returns adjustment** when computing artist royalties on sales activity.
- **Accruing indeterminable club fees in aggregate.** Estimate them licence by licence.

---

## Key terms

- **Advance royalty** — an amount paid to publishers, producers, songwriters, or artists ahead of royalties earned; generally **nonrefundable**.
- **Licence agreement** — a contractual grant of the right to sell or distribute recordings or sheet music for a fixed or sales-based fee.
- **Minimum guarantee** — an amount paid in advance by a licensee to a licensor for distribution rights.
- **Record master** — the master tape from the artist's performance, used to produce molds and tapes for cartridges, cassettes, DVDs, and reel tapes.
- **Royalties** — amounts paid to producers, songwriters, and artists for participation in recordings, and to publishers for copyright interest.
