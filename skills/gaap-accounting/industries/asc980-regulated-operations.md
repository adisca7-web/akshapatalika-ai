# ASC 980 — Regulated Operations

> Industry skill distilled from *Wiley GAAP 2020*, ch. 64 (Specialized Industry GAAP).
> Rules and structure synthesised locally; no source text reproduced.

**Applies primarily to public utilities** — entities whose ability to set selling prices is constrained by government action. The regulatory process is designed to let them recover costs incurred plus a reasonable return to stockholders.

---

## Core model

ASC 980 is the one place in GAAP where **a regulator's decision creates an asset or a liability**. If a regulator will let a utility bill customers later for a cost incurred today, that future rate-recovery right is an asset — even though an unregulated company would have to expense the same cost immediately. The mirror applies: if a regulator will require money to be handed back, that is a liability even where an unregulated company would report a gain.

This works **only while cost-based rate recovery holds**. Application depends on continuing to meet criteria relating to the **intended ability to recover all costs through the rate-setting process**. When those conditions cease — through deregulation, or a shift to rate-setting not based on cost recovery — **application of ASC 980 terminates.**

That conditionality is the whole risk profile of the topic: the assets exist because of a regulatory promise, and deregulation can strand them.

## Subskills

### 1. Recognise regulatory assets
Where costs are **not recognised for current rate-setting purposes but it is probable they will be recovered through future revenue**, capitalise them even though an unregulated entity would expense them currently (ASC 980-340-25-1).

Deferred costs **may include an imputed cost of equity capital** where it is so accounted for in rate-setting — an item GAAP would not normally permit to be recognised at all.

Then maintain it:
- If it becomes apparent the cost **will not** be recovered from future revenue → **charge to expense** (980-340-40-1).
- If a regulator **excludes specific costs** from allowable costs → **reduce the asset's carrying value** by the excluded costs.
- If the regulator **subsequently allows recovery** of previously excluded or additional costs → recognise a **new asset**, classified as if those costs had been included in allowable costs from the start.

### 2. Recognise regulatory liabilities
Three triggers (ASC 980-405-25-1):

| Trigger | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Regulator mandates **refunds to customers** | Accrue when probable and reasonably estimable, per **ASC 450** |
| Rates set **higher to recover expected future costs**, refundable if actual costs come in lower | The incremental rate increase is a **liability (unearned revenue)** until the condition is satisfied |
| Regulator stipulates a **realised gain must be returned** to customers over a future period | **Accrue a liability instead of recognising the gain** |

### 3. Account for abandonments
When an abandonment occurs or **becomes probable** (ASC 980-360-35-1, 35-3):

1. Write off as a loss any costs **probable of not being recovered**.
2. **Additionally**, if the return on the recoverable investment will be **less than the normal rate of return**, accrue a further loss **currently**, measured as the difference between **projected future revenues discounted at the entity's incremental borrowing rate** and the remaining costs to be recovered. This depends on estimates of time until rate increases take effect and how long they remain — revisions flow to **earnings in the period of the new estimate**.

Then, from abandonment until recovery through rate increases, **accrete** the carrying value of the abandoned plant:

| Expectation | Accretion rate | Amortisation during recovery |
|---|---|---|
| **Full** return of investment | The entity's **overall cost of capital**, as permitted for rate-setting | Same basis as permitted for rate-setting |
| **Partial or no** return | The **incremental borrowing rate** — the same rate used to compute the loss accrual | Amounts producing a **constant rate of return** on the unamortised balance |

### 4. Asset retirement obligations
Utilities face substantial retirement costs — nuclear decommissioning, waste disposal, land restoration including strip-mining sites for coal plants. These are governed by **ASC 410-20**, not by ASC 980.

Key boundaries:
- Applies to **legal obligations** associated with retirement of tangible long-lived assets arising from acquisition, construction, development, or normal operation.
- Does **not** apply where only **moral suasion** encourages cleanup — **even if the entity has a history of voluntary cleanup**.
- However, **promissory estoppel can create a legal obligation** absent contractual or statutory requirements.

Where the cost is a legal obligation, recognise the **present value of future expenditures as an added cost of the asset and as a liability at acquisition**, accrete for the passage of time, and depreciate the asset's recorded cost **including estimated future retirement costs**. Estimate increases and decreases are handled differently (see ASC 410 / ASC 360).

### 5. Deregulation and stranded costs
When deregulation legislation is enacted affecting all or part of operations, **cease applying ASC 980 to the affected operations**. Where deregulation arrives via a **rate order**, the order must be **sufficiently detailed** for the entity to determine how it is affected.

Critically, **regulatory assets are not immediately written off.** Instead:
- Evaluate **regulatory cash flows** to determine whether an impairment occurred and whether the portion of the business generating those cash flows remains subject to ASC 980.
- Write the asset off **only if** it is impaired under **ASC 360** criteria **or** ASC 980 no longer applies.
- **Regulatory liabilities are not reclassified into income until the obligation is eliminated by the regulatory authority.**

For newly arising items, apply the same **source-of-cash-flow analysis**: a cost or obligation becomes a regulatory asset or liability once expensed or incurred after ASC 980 is applied to that portion of operations, **if designated for recovery or settlement via regulated cash flows** (ASC 980-20-35-6).

### 6. Revenue: nonutility generators and alternative revenue programs
**Nonutility generators (NUG)** recognise the **lesser** of (ASC 980-605-25):
1. the amount **billable** under the contract, or
2. the **formula-based pricing arrangement** — kilowatt-hours made available in the period × estimated average revenue per kWh over the contract term for the fixed or scheduled price period.

The formula **may not be used if its only purpose is to establish liquidating damages**. A **receivable arises** where amounts billed are less than the formula amount **and** the contract requires a payment to the NUG at the end of the term that is **probable of recovery**.

**Alternative revenue programs** come in **Type A and Type B** (980-605-25-1). Revenues are recognised when the events permitting billing have occurred **and three specified criteria are met** (980-605-25-4). A utility recognising revenue from a program that does not meet the conditions must **amend the plan or change the program** to comply.

### 7. Other postemployment benefits
- If the regulator includes OPEB costs in rates on a **pay-as-you-go basis**, the regulatory asset relating to ASC 715 cost is **not recognised** (980-715-25-4).
- A regulatory asset **is** recognised for the difference between ASC 715 costs and OPEB costs where future revenue will at least offset the deferred cost **and four specified criteria are met**.
- An entity failing the criteria initially **may recognise the regulatory asset in a later period** when they are met (980-715-25-8), with carrying amount **reduced by any impairment**.

## Decision rules

| If | Then | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Cost-based rate recovery criteria still met | Continue applying ASC 980 | 980-10 |
| Deregulation enacted or rate-setting no longer cost-based | **Cease applying ASC 980** to affected operations | 980-20-35-6 |
| Cost deferred for rate-setting and recovery is probable | Capitalise as a regulatory asset | 980-340-25-1 |
| Recovery becomes improbable | Charge to expense | 980-340-40-1 |
| Regulator disallows specific costs | Reduce the regulatory asset accordingly | 980-340 |
| Regulator later allows previously excluded costs | Recognise a **new** asset as if originally allowable | 980-340 |
| Refund to customers is probable and estimable | Accrue a liability (ASC 450) | 980-405-25-1 |
| Rates include recovery of future costs, refundable | Liability (unearned revenue) until condition satisfied | 980-405-25-1 |
| Gain must be returned to customers | Accrue a liability; **do not** recognise the gain | 980-405-25-1 |
| Abandonment occurs or becomes probable | Write off costs probable of non-recovery | 980-360-35-1 |
| Recoverable return < normal rate of return | Accrue additional loss: PV of future revenues at IBR vs remaining costs | 980-360-35-3 |
| Full return expected during recovery | Accrete at overall cost of capital; amortise on the rate-setting basis | 980-360 |
| Partial or no return expected | Accrete at incremental borrowing rate; amortise to a constant rate of return | 980-360 |
| Retirement cost is a legal obligation | ASC 410-20 ARO: PV added to asset and liability, accreted | ASC 410-20 |
| Cleanup driven only by moral suasion | **No ARO**, even with a history of voluntary cleanup | ASC 410-20 |
| Deregulation occurs | Do **not** write off regulatory assets immediately; test under ASC 360 | 980-20-35-6 |
| NUG revenue | Lesser of billable amount and formula-based amount | 980-605-25 |
| Formula exists only for liquidating damages | Do not recognise revenue on the formula | 980-605-25 |
| Regulator recovers OPEB pay-as-you-go | Do not recognise the ASC 715 regulatory asset | 980-715-25-4 |

## Anti-patterns

- **Continuing to recognise regulatory assets after cost-based rate recovery ends.** The criteria are a continuing condition, not a one-time entry test.
- **Writing off all regulatory assets the moment deregulation is announced.** Perform the regulatory-cash-flow and ASC 360 impairment analysis first.
- **Releasing regulatory liabilities into income at deregulation.** They stay until the regulator eliminates the obligation.
- **Recognising a gain the regulator has ordered returned to customers.** Accrue a liability instead.
- **Recording only the non-recovery write-off on abandonment.** A sub-normal return triggers an *additional* discounted loss accrual.
- **Accreting an abandoned plant at the cost of capital when only a partial return is expected.** Use the incremental borrowing rate — the same rate used for the loss.
- **Recognising an ARO for voluntary cleanup.** It must be a legal obligation, though promissory estoppel can create one.
- **Using the NUG formula price when it exceeds the billable amount.** Recognise the lesser.
- **Recognising an OPEB regulatory asset** where the regulator funds OPEB pay-as-you-go.
