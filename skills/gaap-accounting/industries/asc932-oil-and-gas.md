# ASC 932 — Extractive Activities: Oil and Gas

> Industry skill distilled from *Wiley GAAP 2020*, ch. 64 (Specialized Industry GAAP).
> Rules and structure synthesised locally; no source text reproduced.

**Applies to** oil and gas **producing** activities: acquisition of mineral interests in properties, exploration, development, and production of crude oil (including condensate and natural gas liquids) and natural gas.

**Does not address** refining, marketing, or transportation.

---

## Core model

Oil and gas is the classic **"which costs bought an asset?"** problem. Most exploratory spending finds nothing. Two acceptable answers exist, and the choice is an accounting policy that materially changes the balance sheet.

| | **Successful efforts** | **Full cost** |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Traditional historical cost | SEC-prescribed alternative |
| Capitalise | Only costs with a **direct relationship to specific reserves discovered** | Broadly, all exploration costs within a cost centre |
| Costs not tied to specific reserves | **Expensed immediately** — analogous to R&D | Capitalised into the pool |
| Status in GAAP | **Preferred, but not required** | Permitted alternative |

Both methods **must disclose** the method used for accounting for costs incurred and the method of disposition of capitalised costs. The SEC requires additional disclosures for publicly traded companies and, in **SAB 106**, addresses ensuring that expected cash flows for asset retirement obligations are **not double-counted** in full cost computations.

Oil and gas producers are also subject to **ASC 410** asset retirement obligations — well plugging and site restoration are recognised as liabilities with a corresponding asset.

## Subskills

### 1. Capitalise the right asset categories
Costs of the special asset types used in producing activities are capitalised when incurred:
- **Mineral interests in properties** — *unproved* (no proved reserves) and *proved* (with proved reserves)
- **Wells and related equipment and facilities**
- **Support equipment and facilities**
- **Uncompleted wells, equipment, and facilities**

Acquisition costs incurred to obtain properties by purchase, lease, and similar means are capitalised (ASC 932-360-25-7).

### 2. Apply the exploratory well decision
This is the heart of successful efforts (ASC 932-360-25-3, 35-18):

- Drilling costs for exploratory wells (and exploratory-type stratigraphic wells) related directly to specific reserves are **capitalised pending determination**.
- **Proved reserves found** → reclassify to wells and related equipment and facilities.
- **No proved reserves** → charge capitalised drilling costs (less residual/salvage value) **to expense**.
- **Reserves found but not classifiable as proved** → **continue capitalising only if both**:
  1. the reserves found are **sufficient to justify completion as a producing well**, and
  2. the enterprise is **making sufficient progress** assessing the reserves and the economic and operating viability of the project.

Both conditions, and the second is a continuing test — indefinite suspense is not permitted.

### 3. Expense what cannot be tied to reserves
Always expensed under successful efforts:
- **Geological and geophysical (G&G) costs**
- **Carrying costs of unproved properties**
- **Dry hole and bottom hole contributions**

### 4. Handle development, support, and production costs
**Development costs** are incurred to gain access to and prepare well locations, drill and equip development wells, set up production facilities, and provide better recovery systems (ASC 932-360-25-13). Capitalise as **uncompleted equipment and facilities** until drilling or construction is complete (25-14).

**Support equipment** is capitalised and depreciated; that depreciation and other operating costs are **allocated to exploration, development, or production** as appropriate (ASC 932-360-25-15).

**Production (lifting) costs** through field storage, together with DD&A of capitalised acquisition, exploration, and development costs, form the **cost of oil and gas produced**.

### 5. Amortise on units of production
On discovery of proved reserves, reclassify unproved properties to proved, and reclassify uncompleted wells, equipment, and facilities to completed (ASC 932-360-35-2).

Capitalised costs are amortised or depleted by the **unit of production method**, and **estimated residual values must be considered** in setting amortisation and depreciation rates (ASC 932-360-35-3). Amortisation runs as reserves are produced.

### 6. Test for impairment — two different models
A genuine trap: **unproved properties do not use the ASC 350 intangibles model.** Their impairment is assessed **based on the level of established reserves** (ASC 932-350-35-8). If impaired, recognise a loss.

For **wells, related equipment, facilities, and related proved properties**, the **ASC 360** impairment provisions apply — the two-step undiscounted-cash-flow recoverability test, then fair value.

### 7. Interest capitalisation under full cost
Under the full cost method:
- Assets being **depreciated, depleted, or amortised** are considered **in use in earning activities** and therefore **do not qualify** for interest capitalisation.
- Assets employed in **ongoing exploration or development**, not yet in earning activities and **not presently being DD&A'd**, **are** subject to interest capitalisation.

### 8. Wells in progress at period end
If a well or test in progress at the reporting date is found **after period end but before the financial statements are issued** to be unsuccessful, **expense the incurred costs less salvage value**. **Retroactive restatement is not permitted.** Information becoming available before issuance is considered in evaluating conditions existing at the balance sheet date.

## Decision rules

| If | Then | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Cost is property acquisition, proved or unproved | Capitalise as incurred | 932-360-25-7 |
| Cost cannot be tied to specific reserves discovered | Expense immediately (R&D analogue) | 932-10 |
| Cost is G&G, unproved carrying cost, or dry/bottom hole contribution | Expense | 932-360 |
| Exploratory well drilling, outcome undetermined | Capitalise pending determination | 932-360-25-3 |
| Proved reserves found | Reclassify to wells and related equipment | 932-360-25-3 |
| No proved reserves found | Expense drilling costs less residual value | 932-360-25-3 |
| Reserves found, not yet proved | Continue capitalising **only** if completion is justified **and** assessment progress is sufficient | 932-360-35-18 |
| Development cost incurred | Capitalise as uncompleted equipment/facilities until complete | 932-360-25-13/14 |
| Amortising capitalised costs | **Unit of production**, considering residual values | 932-360-35-3 |
| Testing unproved property for impairment | Use the **reserve-level** model, **not** ASC 350 | 932-350-35-8 |
| Testing wells and proved properties | Apply **ASC 360** | 932-360 |
| Full cost, asset currently being DD&A'd | **No** interest capitalisation | — |
| Full cost, asset in exploration/development not yet DD&A'd | Interest capitalisation applies | — |
| Well found unsuccessful after year end, before issuance | Expense costs less salvage; **no restatement** | — |
| Well plugging/site restoration obligation | Recognise an ARO under ASC 410 | ASC 410 |

## Anti-patterns

- **Assuming successful efforts is mandatory.** It is preferred, not required; full cost is a permitted SEC-prescribed alternative. Whichever is used must be disclosed.
- **Leaving unsuccessful exploratory well costs in suspense indefinitely.** Continued capitalisation requires both the completion-justification and sufficient-progress tests, tested continuously.
- **Capitalising G&G costs** under successful efforts.
- **Applying the ASC 350 intangibles impairment model to unproved properties.** Their model is reserve-level based.
- **Capitalising interest on producing (DD&A'd) properties under full cost.** Those assets are already in earning activities.
- **Restating prior statements** when a well is found dry after year end but before issuance. Expense in the current period.
- **Ignoring residual values** in unit-of-production rates.
- **Double-counting ARO cash flows** in full cost ceiling computations — the specific concern of SAB 106.
