# ASC 912 — Contractors: Federal Government

> Industry skill distilled from *Wiley GAAP 2020*, ch. 64 (Specialized Industry GAAP).
> Rules and structure synthesised locally; no source text reproduced.

**Applies to** (ASC 912-10-15-3):
- Cost-plus-fixed-fee contracts
- Contracts **terminated for the convenience of the government**
- Fixed-price war and defense of supply contracts

Covers both **prime contracts** (direct with the U.S. government) and **subcontracts** performed indirectly under a prime contractor's supervision and control.

---

## Core model

Two features distinguish government contracting from ordinary ASC 606 work, and both stem from the government's unilateral power over the contract.

1. **Fees are earned by performance, not by billing.** Under a cost-plus-fixed-fee contract, income is recognised on **partial performance** where there is **reasonable assurance of realisation**. Fees are also accrued as they become billable — **unless that accrual is not reasonably related to the proportionate performance** of the total work. Billability is a permitted proxy for performance, overridden the moment it stops tracking performance.

2. **The government can terminate for its own convenience.** That is not a breach and not a loss event. The contractor is entitled to reimbursement of expenditures plus an appropriate portion of the fixed fee, and the resulting **termination claim** is an asset with its own presentation rules.

All federal contracts and modifications negotiated on a cost basis with commercial entities must follow the cost principles mandated by **Federal Acquisition Legislation and Regulations** in pricing (ASC 912-20-05-2). Those cost principles govern what is billable; GAAP governs what is recognised. They are not the same set and should not be conflated.

## Subskills

### 1. Recognise cost-plus-fixed-fee income
The flat-fee payment amount is usually determined by the ratio of actual expenditures to total estimated expenditures — a cost-to-cost measure of progress.

Recognise the fee on partial performance **if realisation is reasonably assured**. Accrue as billable, except where billing does not reasonably track proportionate performance.

### 2. Treat advances as liabilities
Advances under a CPFF contract are generally intended to help the contractor **finance its costs**. Therefore:
- **Default: advances are liabilities and are not offset against contract receivables.**
- Offset is permitted **only** where there is an expectation the advances will be applied against those **specific** charges.
- Any such offset **requires disclosure** in the financial statements.

This is a deliberately narrow exception. Netting financing advances against receivables would overstate neither total assets nor liabilities but would understate both, obscuring the contractor's true gross position.

### 3. Account for convenience terminations
When the government terminates for its convenience (ASC 912-20-05-4):
- **Material termination claims are separately captioned in the revenues section** of the income statement (ASC 912-20-45-5). They are not buried in contract revenue.
- Inventory **reacquired by the contractor** after having been included in a termination claim is **recorded as a purchase** (ASC 912-330-25-1), and the credit is **applied against the termination claim receivable** (ASC 912-310-35-1).

### 4. Handle disposal credits and no-cost settlements
**Disposal credits** reduce the termination claim receivable where the contractor retains, or sells to outsiders, some or all of the termination inventory covered by the claim. For items retained as scrap or for the contractor's own use, the credit amount is **determined by agreement between the contractor and a government representative** — a negotiated figure, not a unilateral estimate.

**No-cost settlements** arise where the contractor prefers to keep termination inventory for other production or disposal at its own risk and makes no claim at all. Then (ASC 912-310-25-5):
- There is **no sale** of inventory or other items to the government.
- There is **no occasion to accrue any profit** from the termination.
- Costs otherwise applicable to the contract receive their **usual treatment**.
- Retained inventory or property, already recorded, requires **no charge to purchases** and follows the usual procedures for such assets.

The trap is symmetrical to the reacquisition rule: reacquiring claimed inventory *is* a purchase; retaining inventory under a no-cost settlement is *not*.

### 5. Subcontractor claims
Obligations of a contractor to a subcontractor arising from subcontractor costs incurred on a terminated contract, where those transactions **did not result in transfer of billable materials or services** to the contractor before termination.

## Decision rules

| If | Then | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| CPFF fee, realisation reasonably assured | Recognise on partial performance | 912-10-15-3 |
| Fee is billable but billing does not track performance | Do **not** accrue on the billing basis | 912-10-15-3 |
| Advance received to finance costs | Liability; do not offset receivables | — |
| Advance expected to apply to specific charges | Offset permitted, **with disclosure** | — |
| Contract terminated for convenience | Claim reimbursement of costs plus appropriate portion of fixed fee | 912-20-05-4 |
| Termination claim is material | Separately caption in the revenues section | 912-20-45-5 |
| Contractor reacquires inventory included in a claim | Record as a **purchase**; credit the claim receivable | 912-330-25-1, 912-310-35-1 |
| Contractor retains inventory as scrap or for own use | Disposal credit set **by agreement** with the government | 912-310-25-4 |
| Contractor makes no claim (no-cost settlement) | No sale, **no profit accrual**, no charge to purchases | 912-310-25-5 |
| Pricing a negotiated cost-based contract | Follow FAR cost principles (billing), separately from GAAP (recognition) | 912-20-05-2 |

## Anti-patterns

- **Recognising fees as billed** when billing milestones run ahead of actual performance.
- **Netting government advances against contract receivables** without a specific-charge expectation, or without disclosing the offset.
- **Treating a convenience termination as a loss contingency.** It is a contractual entitlement to reimbursement plus fee.
- **Burying a material termination claim in ordinary contract revenue.** It gets its own caption.
- **Accruing profit on a no-cost settlement.** There is no sale, so there is no profit.
- **Charging retained no-cost-settlement inventory to purchases.** Only reacquired *claimed* inventory is a purchase.
- **Assuming FAR-allowable equals GAAP-recognisable.** FAR governs what may be billed; GAAP governs what may be recognised.

## Key terms

- **Contract** — a legal agreement obligating a prime contractor to provide products or services to the U.S. government; also subcontracts obligating subcontractors to perform indirectly under the prime's supervision and control.
- **Cost-plus-fixed-fee contract** — the contractor is reimbursed for costs plus a fixed fee.
- **Disposal credits** — amounts deducted from a termination claim receivable because the contractor retained or sold termination inventory.
- **No-cost settlement** — a termination in which the contractor makes no claim, retaining inventory at its own risk.
- **Subcontractor's claims** — obligations to a subcontractor for costs on a terminated contract that never produced billable transfers to the contractor.
