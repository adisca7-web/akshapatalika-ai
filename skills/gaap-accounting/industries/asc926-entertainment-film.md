# ASC 926 — Entertainment: Films

> Industry skill distilled from *Wiley GAAP 2020*, ch. 64 (Specialized Industry GAAP).
> Rules and structure synthesised locally; no source text reproduced.

**Applies to** producers and distributors who own or hold rights to distribute or exploit films — feature films, television series, television specials, animated films, and similar programming, on any format (celluloid, videotape, digital). (ASC 926-10-15-2)

**Does not apply to** the recording industry (ASC 928), cable television (ASC 922), broadcasters (ASC 920), software to be sold or leased (ASC 985-20), or entertainment and educational software (ASC 985-705).

---

## Core model

A film is a capitalised asset amortised against the revenue it is *forecast* to earn over its whole life, not over a fixed period. Because a film's earning pattern is front-loaded and wildly uncertain, straight-line amortisation would misstate margin badly. ASC 926 instead ties cost recognition to revenue realisation through one ratio, applied to both film costs and participation costs so that margin stays constant.

**Individual-film-forecast computation method** (ASC 926-20-35-1):

```
                current period actual revenue
expense  =  ─────────────────────────────────────────  ×  unamortised balance
            remaining ultimate revenue at the
            beginning of the fiscal year
```

Applied to unamortised film costs, it gives amortisation. Applied to unaccrued ultimate participation costs, it gives the participation accrual. Absent estimate changes, this yields a **constant rate of profit** across the film's life before exploitation, manufacturing, and other period costs.

Amortisation and participation accrual **begin when the film is released and revenue recognition starts** — not when production ends.

## Subskills

### 1. Classify the cost
Four buckets, each with a different fate. Getting this wrong is the most common error, because only the first is capitalised.

| Cost type | Contents | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Film costs** | Direct negative costs, production overhead, production-period interest capitalised per ASC 835-20 | Capitalise; present separately; **noncurrent** asset on a classified balance sheet |
| **Participation costs** | Contingent compensation to talent and rights licensors — participations (contractual formulas) and residuals (collective bargaining) | Accrue by the forecast method, subject to the floor below |
| **Exploitation costs** | Marketing, publicity, promotion, distribution | Advertising follows ASC 720-35; **all others expensed as incurred** (ASC 926-720-25-3) |
| **Manufacturing costs** | Theatrical prints; duplication of sale product (DVD, etc.) | Prints expensed over the period benefited; sale product expensed **unit-specific when its revenue is recognised** (ASC 926-330-35-1) |

Direct negative costs include rights acquisition (books, plays, screenplays), adaptation and development, compensation of cast, directors, producers and staff, set construction and operations, wardrobe, sound synchronisation, location rental, and post-production (music, effects, editing).

Production overhead means the cost of individuals or departments with exclusive or significant responsibility for film production. **It excludes** general and administrative expense, the cost of certain overall deals, and charges for losses on properties sold or abandoned.

### 2. Estimate ultimate revenue
The denominator drives everything, so ASC 926-20-35-5 fences it in tightly. Ultimate revenue is a **gross, undiscounted** amount, excludes projected inflation, and translates foreign-currency portions at **current spot rates** (ASC 926-20-35-8).

**Time limits**
- Ten years from the film's initial release.
- Twenty years from acquisition for a film acquired as part of a **film library** — and a film only qualifies as library if it was initially released **at least three years before** the library acquisition date.
- Episodic television: ten years from delivery of the **first** episode; if the series is still in production, five years from delivery of the **most recent** episode, if later.

**Include only with evidence**
- A market or territory: only where persuasive evidence exists that revenue will be recognised, or the entity can demonstrate a history of recognised revenue there. Newly developing territories require an existing arrangement.
- Merchandise licensing: only with persuasive evidence for that particular film (a signed nonrefundable minimum guarantee or advance) or a demonstrated history of that arrangement type.
- Peripheral items (toys, apparel) sold by the entity itself: only with a history of such revenue on similar films — and then only the **net licence fee equivalent** the entity would have recognised had it licensed the rights, never gross wholesale or retail proceeds.

**Always exclude**
- Revenue from unproven or undeveloped technologies.
- Wholesale promotion and advertising reimbursements from third parties — these **offset exploitation costs** instead.
- Anything beyond the time limits above.

**Do not discount** ultimate revenue to present value unless a significant financing component is identified in a licence contract within ASC 606's scope (ASC 926-20-35-6).

### 3. Accrue participation costs
Run the forecast ratio, then apply two overrides that the formula alone will not give you:
- Accrue **only if it is probable** that assets will be sacrificed to settle the obligation.
- At every balance sheet date, the accrued balance must **at least equal the amount currently payable**. The formula result is a floor-adjusted figure, not the final answer.

### 4. Revise estimates prospectively
Estimates will move. At each reporting date, revise ultimate revenue and ultimate participation costs to current information, then:
- Reset the **denominator** to remaining ultimate revenue as of the **beginning of the fiscal year of change**.
- Leave the **numerator** alone — it is actual results.
- Apply the revised fraction to net unamortised film costs and unaccrued ultimate participation costs.
- Charge or credit the difference between the new-estimate expense and amounts already recorded that fiscal year to income **in the period of revision** (e.g. the quarter).

This is a prospective change in estimate as of the start of the year, not a restatement.

### 5. Test unamortised film costs for impairment
Triggered when there is an indication that fair value of a film monetised on its own, or of a film group, may be below unamortised cost (ASC 926-20-35-12).

**Indicators** (ASC 926-20-35-12A): adverse change in expected performance before release; actual costs substantially over budget; a change in the predominant monetisation strategy so the film is monetised with other films or licence agreements; substantial delays in completion or release; reduced initial release pattern; insufficient funding to complete and market; post-release performance below pre-release expectations.

**Measurement**: write off any excess of unamortised cost over fair value as an operating expense. **Write-offs are never restored** (ASC 926-20-35-13).

Fair value is usually a discounted cash flow estimate. Critically, **the ten- and twenty-year ultimate-revenue limits do not apply** to the cash flows used for fair value (ASC 926-20-35-14) — they constrain the amortisation denominator only. Management considers prior-market performance, public perception of story/cast/director/producer, historical results of similar films and of the same talent, and running time, and must also estimate costs to complete, future exploitation and participation costs, and other outflows (ASC 926-20-35-15).

Under the traditional DCF method use most-likely cash flows with a risk-adjusted discount rate (ASC 926-20-35-16). Under the expected cash flow approach — preferred by ASC 360 — probability-weight all relevant outcomes per period and risk-adjust the rate **only if the expected flows are not already risk-adjusted** (ASC 926-20-35-17).

## Decision rules

| If | Then | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Cost is a direct negative cost, production overhead, or production-period interest | Capitalise as film cost, noncurrent | 926-20 |
| Cost is G&A, an overall deal, or a loss on property abandoned | Expense; not production overhead | 926-20 |
| Cost is marketing, publicity, promotion, distribution | Expense as incurred (advertising per ASC 720-35) | 926-720-25-2/3 |
| Making theatrical prints | Expense over the period benefited | 926-330-35-1 |
| Duplicating DVDs held for sale | Expense unit-specific when that unit's revenue is recognised | 926-330-35-1 |
| Film released and earning revenue | Start film cost amortisation and participation accrual | 926-20-35-1 |
| Estimate of ultimate revenue changes | Reset denominator to start of current fiscal year; adjust in the period of change | 926-20-35-4 |
| Participation payable now exceeds formula accrual | Accrue the payable amount (floor) | 926-405-35-1 |
| Sacrifice of assets for participations is not probable | Do not accrue | 926-405-35-1 |
| Impairment indicator present | Compare fair value to unamortised cost; write off excess | 926-20-35-12/13 |
| Film value recovers after write-down | No restoration | 926-20-35-13 |
| Estimating fair value for impairment | Ignore the 10/20-year revenue limits | 926-20-35-14 |

## Thresholds and limits

| Item | Limit |
|---|---|
| Ultimate revenue horizon, new film | 10 years from initial release |
| Ultimate revenue horizon, acquired film library | 20 years from acquisition |
| Qualifying as "library" | Initially released ≥ 3 years before acquisition |
| Episodic TV series | 10 years from first episode delivery |
| Episodic TV still in production | 5 years from most recent episode delivery, if later |
| "Set for production" | Funding committed, active pre-production begun, principal photography expected within **6 months** |

## Anti-patterns

- **Straight-lining film cost amortisation.** The forecast method exists because film revenue is front-loaded; a straight line overstates early margin and strands cost at the end.
- **Leaving the denominator at the original estimate.** After a revision, the denominator is remaining ultimate revenue at the *beginning of the year of change*. Reusing the original figure double-counts revenue already earned.
- **Restating prior periods for an estimate change.** The change is prospective and lands in the period of revision.
- **Discounting ultimate revenue.** It is a gross undiscounted amount unless ASC 606 identifies a significant financing component.
- **Applying the 10-year cap to the impairment DCF.** The cap governs amortisation only; fair value uses the film's full expected life.
- **Including gross merchandise proceeds in ultimate revenue.** Only the net licence-fee equivalent belongs there.
- **Treating third-party advertising reimbursements as revenue.** They offset exploitation costs.
- **Restoring a previous write-down** when a film outperforms. Prohibited.
- **Accruing participations purely by formula** when the amount currently payable is higher, or when a sacrifice of assets is not probable.

## Key terms

- **Cross-collateralized** — rights to multiple films, territories, or markets granted together, with exploitation results aggregated by the licensee when computing amounts payable to the licensor.
- **Exploitation costs** — all direct costs of distribution: marketing, advertising, publicity, promotion.
- **Film costs** — direct negative costs of physical production plus allocated production overhead and capitalised interest.
- **Market** — a distribution channel in a geographic territory for a media type (theatrical, home video, pay TV, free TV, merchandise licensing).
- **Nonrefundable minimum guarantee** — the guaranteed floor fee in a variable-fee arrangement, including binding commitments to pay over the licence period.
- **Overall deal** — compensation for exclusive or preferential use of a creative individual's services.
- **Participation costs** — contingent compensation to participants via participations (formulas) and residuals (collective bargaining).
- **Sale** — transfer of control of the master copy and all associated rights.
- **Territory** — the geographic area of exploitation, usually a country, sometimes a common-language group.
- **Ultimate revenue** — total estimated revenue from all markets and territories over the permitted horizon.
