# ASC 976 — Real Estate: Retail Land

> Industry skill distilled from *Wiley GAAP 2020*, ch. 64 (Specialized Industry GAAP).
> Rules and structure synthesised locally; no source text reproduced.

> **Coverage note.** This is one of the briefest sections in the book (264 words). Wiley treats ASC 976 as a scope-and-cross-reference topic, because most retail land sale mechanics now run through ASC 606 and the receivables guidance in ASC 310. Treat this skill as a **routing and scope** aid, not a complete treatment — for measurement, go to `ch38-revenue-from-contracts-with-customers` and `ch16-receivables`.

---

## Scope

**Applies to** all entities that sell, **on a volume basis**, lots that are subdivisions of large tracts of land (ASC 976-10-15-3). These sales are characterised by **small down payments and a sales contract for the balance** (ASC 976-10-05-2) — that is, heavy seller financing against a thin equity cushion.

ASC 976 provides guidance on **receivables, inventory, and cost of sales and services**.

**Does not apply to** (ASC 976-10-15-4) sales of:
- Lots **to builders**
- Homes, buildings, and parcels of land to builders and others
- Corporate stock of entities with substantial real estate, partnership interests, and time-sharing interests where the sales are **in-substance sales of real estate**
- **Options** to purchase real estate

The scope line worth remembering: **selling lots to consumers in volume is in scope; selling lots to builders is not.** The regime exists because of the retail buyer's small down payment and high cancellation rate, which do not characterise builder sales.

---

## Core model

The economics mirror time-sharing (ASC 978): volume, homogeneity, seller financing, and a meaningful rate of buyer cancellation. Because a small down payment gives the buyer little incentive to complete, **cancellation is expected, not exceptional**, and the accounting is built around an allowance rather than case-by-case write-offs.

**On cancellation, receivable balances are charged to the allowance for contract cancellation.** The allowance is established up front against expected cancellations; individual cancellations then flow through it without hitting income.

## Decision rules

| If | Then | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Selling subdivided lots to consumers on a volume basis | In scope for ASC 976 receivables, inventory, and cost of sales | 976-10-15-3 |
| Selling lots to **builders** | **Out of scope** — ordinary revenue and receivables guidance | 976-10-15-4 |
| Selling stock, partnership, or time-share interests that are in-substance real estate sales | Out of scope of ASC 976 | 976-10-15-4 |
| Selling an option to purchase real estate | Out of scope | 976-10-15-4 |
| A contract is cancelled | Charge the receivable balance to the **allowance for contract cancellation** | 976 |
| Measuring the revenue itself | Apply **ASC 606**; ASC 976 is incremental | — |

---

## Anti-patterns

- **Writing cancelled contracts off directly to bad debt expense.** They are charged to the allowance for contract cancellation, which should already anticipate them.
- **Failing to establish a cancellation allowance** in a small-down-payment, volume-sale model where cancellation is structural.
- **Applying ASC 976 to builder sales.** Explicitly excluded.
- **Applying it to in-substance real estate sales** effected through stock, partnership, or time-share interests.
- **Treating ASC 976 as a complete revenue model.** It is incremental to ASC 606.

## Key terms

- **Lien** — a claim or charge a creditor has on property serving as security for payment of a debt.
- **Retail land sale** — a volume sale of subdivided lots characterised by a small down payment and a contract for the balance.
